Repair first

A repair is the right call more often than you'd think

Roofing companies make more money replacing roofs than fixing them, and homeowners know it. That is why so many people in Delaware County put off calling about a few blown-off shingles or a stain on the ceiling – they expect to be told they need a whole new roof. We work the other way around. Our starting assumption is that the roof can be repaired, and we only recommend a replacement when the inspection shows the roof is failing as a system rather than at a few points.

Here, most repair calls come from two very different kinds of houses. The first is the production-built home from the 1990s and 2000s boom in Lewis Center and Orange Township, Genoa, Liberty and Concord townships – roofs that are 18 to 28 years old with specific, predictable weak points. The second is the older home in the historic cores of the City of Delaware, Sunbury, Galena, Ashley and Ostrander, where the roof has been patched, added onto and re-covered over many decades. The repair approach for each is different, and we treat them differently.

If water is already coming in, our roof leak repair page explains how we trace the source. For anything else, call (740) 555-0177 and we will come take a look at no charge.

What we fix

Common roof repairs we handle

Most repairs are completed in a single visit. We match shingle color and profile as closely as current products allow and photograph the work before and after so you have a record for your files – or for an insurance adjuster if the damage came from a storm.

  • Blown-off, creased or lifted shingles after a wind event, including re-sealing tabs that have let go
  • Cracked or dried-out rubber pipe boots around plumbing vents
  • Step flashing and counter flashing at walls, dormers and chimneys
  • Ridge cap and hip shingles that have cracked or blown loose
  • Exposed or backed-out nails that have punched up through the shingles
  • Damaged drip edge, starter strip and rake edges
  • Attic vent, box vent and ridge vent replacement
  • Small decking repairs beneath localized damage
On the job

We see a lot of this in Orange Township – the rubber collars on the plumbing vent boots dry out and split long before the shingles around them give up. On houses from the 2004 permit peak, a cracked boot is one of the most common repair calls we get: a small brown ring on the ceiling under a bathroom, and a roof that is otherwise holding together. A new boot costs a fraction of what people fear, and it often buys the rest of the roof several more seasons.

Local conditions

Why Delaware County roofs break where they do

Wind is the main culprit. Large stretches of the county were farm fields until the subdivisions arrived, and in Liberty Township alone about 71 percent of the land is zoned for one-acre farm-residence lots. With few mature trees to slow it down, wind hits the rakes, ridges and corners of these roofs at full strength. Once a shingle's seal strip lets go, every storm after that flexes it until it creases or tears off.

The county has seen what serious wind does. The March 14, 2024 EF1 tornado – up to 110 mph – tracked from south of Delaware through Berlin Township and on toward Sunbury and Galena, and EF0 tornadoes struck near Delaware Lake in 2016 and east of the city in 2020. Central Ohio also took widespread wind from the June 2012 derecho and the 2008 remnants of Hurricane Ike. Roofs that come through a big wind event with only a few missing shingles often get a quick patch and are forgotten – and those quick patches are a big share of what we end up repairing properly.

Age compounds it. Builder-grade shingles from the late 1990s and 2000s lose granules and flexibility as they age, so a seal that held for 15 years starts to fail all at once. If your roof was installed during Genoa Township's 1998–2003 building wave, read our guide on why 1998–2008 subdivision roofs are failing now before you decide how much to spend on repairs.

On the job

In our experience with older homes in Galena and Ashley , the trouble spot is rarely the middle of the roof – it is the seam where an addition, porch or back kitchen was tied into the original house decades after it was built. Two roof planes built at different times, often at slightly different pitches, meet in a valley or against a wall with improvised flashing. Re-flashing that one junction properly is usually a far better use of money than re-roofing a village house that is otherwise sound.

The honest answer

When we'll tell you to stop repairing

A repair makes sense when the damage is localized and the rest of the roof still has life in it. It stops making sense when we find widespread granule loss, shingles that crack when lifted, seal strips that have let go across whole slopes, soft decking in several places, or a roof that has already been repaired several times. At that point you are paying to patch a system that is wearing out everywhere at once.

When we reach that conclusion, we show you the photos and explain why. Then the choice is yours – and if a replacement is the right move, our roof replacement page explains what a full tear-off includes and our Delaware County roof replacement cost guide gives typical price ranges.

Repairs that involve storm damage are worth documenting carefully. If the damage came from wind or hail, our storm damage roof repair service includes photo documentation for your insurance claim. Small repairs generally do not require a building permit, but larger re-covering work does under Ohio's Residential Code – our Delaware County roof permit guide explains where that line falls and which office handles your address.

Frequently asked

Can you match the shingles on my 20-year-old roof?

We get as close as current products allow. Many builder-grade colors from the early 2000s have been discontinued or reformulated, and older shingles have faded, so a repair may be visible up close. On a back slope that rarely matters; on a front elevation we will tell you before we start.

How do I know whether my roof needs a repair or a replacement?

Localized damage on an otherwise healthy roof is a repair. Widespread granule loss, brittle shingles, failing seal strips across whole slopes or repeated leaks point to replacement. Our free inspection includes photos so you can see the condition yourself. Call (740) 555-0177 to schedule.

Do I need a permit for a roof repair in Delaware County?

Minor repairs generally do not, but Ohio's Board of Building Standards requires approval for re-roofing work that goes beyond a minor repair. We confirm with the correct office – County Building Safety, the City of Delaware, Powell or Westerville – when a job is on the line.

Some shingles blew off in a storm. Should I file an insurance claim?

It depends on the extent of the damage and your deductible. We document what we find with photos and give you an honest read on whether the damage is likely to exceed your deductible before you decide. See our roof insurance claims guide.

How quickly can you repair a few missing shingles?

Small repairs are usually scheduled within days and finished in one visit. After a major county-wide storm, demand spikes, so if the decking is exposed we can tarp the area first through our emergency roof tarping service.

Do you repair older roofs in the historic part of Delaware?

Yes. Older homes need a repair-first approach, and roof work in the City of Delaware's Downtown Historic District requires a Certificate of Appropriateness before work begins. We help with that application.
