About Ashley

Roofing for a northern Delaware County village

Ashley is a small village in Oxford Township in northern Delaware County. It was laid out in 1849 and named for L. W. Ashley, and the 2020 census counted 1,198 residents, with a 2023 estimate of 1,318. Families here are served by Buckeye Valley schools and the Elm Valley Joint Fire District, and the village is surrounded by the open countryside of Oxford Township — a very different setting from the subdivisions farther south in the county.

We won't pretend every Ashley home is the same, because they aren't. A village that's been around since 1849 can have houses from many different eras on the same block, and each one may have been re-roofed, patched or added onto several times by different owners. That's why we treat every Ashley roof as its own case. We don't quote from a satellite photo or assume what's under the shingles; we get on the roof, go into the attic when we can, and show you photos of what we actually find.

For many homes that inspection leads to a targeted roof repair rather than a full replacement — a section of failed shingles, a leaking chimney or vent, a worn valley. When the roof is truly at the end of its life, we'll explain why and walk you through a full roof replacement, including what the Residential Code of Ohio requires once the old roofing comes off.

On the job

When we inspect an older house in a village like Ashley , one of the first things we check is how many layers of roofing are already up there. Over the life of a house, it's common for someone to have laid new shingles right over the old ones to save the tear-off. The Residential Code of Ohio doesn't allow a new covering over a roof that already has two or more layers, so the layer count changes the whole scope of the job — and it's something you want to know before you get a price, not after the crew arrives.

Weather exposure

Open-country wind and central Ohio winters

Ashley sits amid farm fields and open ground, and roofs out here don't get much shelter. Wind across open country is hard on shingle seal strips, ridge caps and roof edges, and it's often the exposed slope facing the fields that wears first. Central Ohio has a long record of damaging wind: the June 29, 2012 derecho brought gusts of 70–80+ mph across the region, and the remnants of Hurricane Ike on September 14, 2008 pushed a 75 mph gust through the Columbus airport. Delaware County itself has seen several tornadoes in the last decade, including the March 14, 2024 EF1 that crossed the middle of the county with winds up to 110 mph.

Winter is the slower threat. Columbus-area January temperatures average 29.6°F, with about 28 inches of snow a year and long stretches hovering around freezing. Snow that melts and refreezes at the eaves can back water up under shingles, which is why the Residential Code of Ohio requires an ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line. On older homes, the eaves are one of the first places we look.

Permits

Who permits a re-roof in Ashley?

Ashley doesn't run its own residential building department. According to Delaware County Building Safety's jurisdiction table, residential permits for the Village of Ashley are issued by Delaware County Building Safety — the same office that handles the county's townships. The county building code requires a permit to alter, repair or replace, and the Ohio Board of Building Standards has stated that plan approval is required for reroofing one-, two- and three-family dwellings unless the work is a minor repair.

In plain terms: if you're replacing the roof, it needs a permit, and a reputable contractor pulls it. We file with the county, schedule the inspections and keep the paperwork with your job file. Our Delaware County re-roof permit guide explains how permitting works across the county if you're curious about the details.

Frequently asked — Ashley

Does the Village of Ashley issue its own roofing permits?

Residential permits for Ashley are issued by Delaware County Building Safety, per the county's jurisdiction table. We pull the county permit and schedule the inspections for you.

My older Ashley home may have more than one layer of shingles. Can you roof over it?

Only if there's a single existing layer and the roof deck is sound. The Residential Code of Ohio doesn't allow a new covering over a roof with two or more layers, so those roofs need a full tear-off. A free roof inspection confirms the layer count.

Do you repair barn and outbuilding roofs near Ashley?

Yes. We inspect and repair outbuilding roofs and can quote metal roofing for barns, garages and sheds. Call (740) 555-0177 to set up a visit.

Will you recommend repair instead of replacement if that's all my Ashley roof needs?

Yes. We start every inspection by asking whether a targeted roof repair will do the job, and we show you photos so you can see why.

Why does the ice barrier matter for a roof in northern Delaware County?

Central Ohio winters hover around freezing, and melting snow can refreeze at the eaves and back water under shingles. The Residential Code of Ohio requires an ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line, and we install it on every re-roof.
