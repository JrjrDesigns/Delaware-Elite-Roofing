Housing stock

Ohio's fastest-growing county is now reaching re-roof age

Delaware County grew from 66,929 residents in 1990 to 214,124 in 2020, and it was the fastest-growing county in Ohio by percentage in each of the last three census decades. That growth was not spread evenly over time. The southern townships filled in with subdivisions during the late 1990s and 2000s: Orange Township averaged about 405 new-home permits a year from 1995 on and peaked at 762 in 2004, and Genoa Township permitted 3,810 new dwellings between 1998 and 2003 alone. Homes built in one rush tend to wear out their roofs in one rush, and that is exactly what is happening now.

Most of those houses went up with the builder-standard shingles of the day, and those roofs are now roughly 18 to 28 years old. Our guide on why 1998–2008 builder-grade roofs are failing now explains what to look for, and it reads very differently in a 2004 Orange Township colonial than in a 2023 Berlin Township new build. In the southern half of the county — Lewis Center, Genoa Township, Liberty Township and Concord Township — first-generation roofs are the bulk of the work we see.

The county is not all subdivisions. The City of Delaware has a downtown historic district and more than 500 National Register homes and carriage houses in its Historic Northwest Neighborhood, and the northern and western villages date to the 1800s. Many unincorporated areas were originally zoned for 1- to 5-acre lots, so plenty of homes sit on open former farmland with few mature trees to break the wind. When it is time for a new roof, our roof replacement process starts with a free inspection so you know whether a repair still makes sense. Across central Ohio, most asphalt re-roofs land somewhere in the high four figures to the mid-teens depending on size, pitch and tear-off, but the free inspection is what gives you a real number for your house.

Storm exposure

Tornado tracks and windstorms that have crossed Delaware County

The defining roofing event of recent years is the March 14, 2024 EF1 tornado. According to the National Weather Service, it formed near US-23 south of Delaware at about 8:58 p.m., tracked through Berlin Township, crossed the Olentangy River, passed near Sunbury and Galena and continued into Licking County — a path of roughly 36 miles, up to 600 yards wide, with peak winds of 110 mph. NWS surveyors found partial roof removal in some neighborhoods, and the Delaware Gazette reported at least 254 homes and businesses affected, 25 with major damage, before a state emergency was declared. That same night, a separate tornado that began in Union County entered western Delaware County as an EF1 and moved east toward Marysville Road.

Earlier, smaller tornadoes left their own marks: an EF0 on August 20, 2016 near Delaware Lake and the Delaware Dam, and an EF0 on September 7, 2020 that ran from east of Delaware to northwest of Sunbury and partially lifted a home's roof. Countywide, homes also rode out the central Ohio windstorms of September 2008 (the remnants of Hurricane Ike) and the June 29, 2012 derecho, when gusts of 70–80+ mph swept the region. If your roof was patched in a hurry after any of these, our storm damage roof repair team can check whether the fix has held, and the after-a-storm checklist walks you through what to photograph first.

Homes in Berlin Township, Sunbury and Galena sat closest to the 2024 track. If you have an open roof right now, we offer emergency roof tarping — call (740) 555-0177 day or night.

On the job

We see a lot of this across the 2024 tornado path — roofs that were patched quickly that spring, when every crew in the region was booked, and never looked at again. On follow-up inspections we find replacement shingles that don't match the rest of the slope, nails left exposed where a tab was face-nailed, and ridge cap that was reset but never sealed. None of it leaks on day one. It usually shows up a few seasons later as a stain on a bedroom ceiling, which is why we tell homeowners on the path that a second look is worth the time.

Permits & approvals

Four permit offices, one county: who approves your re-roof

Under the Residential Code of Ohio, re-roofing a house generally needs building-department approval unless it counts as a minor repair, and Delaware County has several departments. Delaware County Building Safety issues residential permits for every township and for Sunbury, Galena, Ashley, Ostrander and Shawnee Hills. The City of Delaware, the City of Powell and the City of Westerville run their own building departments, and pockets of the county near Polaris and Dublin are permitted by Columbus or Dublin instead. We pull the permit from whichever office has your address; the full breakdown is in our guide to Delaware County roof permits.

A few local wrinkles matter. Powell requires a permit for every re-roof and asks for photos of the ice guard, felt, flashing and valleys. Downtown Delaware's historic district requires a Certificate of Appropriateness before roof work starts. Concord Township does not need a township zoning permit for roof replacement, but the county building permit still applies. Ostrander issues its own village permits and requires a county permit for permanent structural items, and Ashley is handled by the county office.

On the job

When homeowners ask us who issues their permit — the mailing address is usually the first thing that throws them off. A Delaware, Powell or Lewis Center address does not tell you which building department has the property; township lines, annexations and city limits do. We look up the parcel before we quote, because a permit filed with the wrong office is a delay nobody needs in the middle of a tear-off.

HOAs

HOA and design-review approvals

Several Delaware County communities have design review boards. The Scioto Reserve HOA in Concord Township says all roof replacements must be applied for and approved before installation, with Driftwood/Weathered Wood as the original community shingle color. Evans Farm requires Design Review Committee approval for exterior changes, and Golf Village in Powell has its own Design Review Board. Our HOA-approved roof replacement service assembles the color, material and product packet your board wants to see, so the approval is in hand before materials are ordered.

Frequently asked — Delaware County

Do you serve all of Delaware County or just the southern suburbs?

All of it. We work in the cities of Delaware, Powell and Sunbury, the villages of Galena, Ashley and Ostrander, and every township from Concord to Genoa. Call (740) 555-0177 or see our service areas page.

Which office issues my roofing permit in Delaware County?

Delaware County Building Safety covers the townships plus Sunbury, Galena, Ashley, Ostrander and Shawnee Hills. The City of Delaware, Powell and Westerville run their own departments. We confirm the right office from your parcel and pull the permit for you — details are in our permit guide.

My roof was repaired after the March 2024 tornado. Should I have it checked again?

It is worth it if the work was done in a rush that spring. Quick patches can hide mismatched or unsealed shingles that leak seasons later. A free roof inspection will tell you whether the repair is holding.

How long do the original roofs in 2000s Delaware County subdivisions last?

Many builder-standard roofs installed during the 1998–2008 boom are now 18 to 28 years old and showing granule loss, cracked seals and worn valleys. Our builder-grade roof guide covers the warning signs, and an inspection gives you an honest repair-or-replace answer.

Can you handle the insurance claim if a storm damages my roof?

We document the damage with photos and measurements, meet your adjuster on site and provide the paperwork your insurer needs. Under Ohio rules, insurers must acknowledge a claim within 15 days. Call (740) 555-0177 after any major storm.
