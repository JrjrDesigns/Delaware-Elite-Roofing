Why it matters here

Delaware County Storms Are Not Hypothetical

On the night of March 14, 2024, an EF1 tornado formed near US-23 south of Delaware a little before 9 p.m. The National Weather Service found it reached 110 mph, ran roughly 36 miles and was up to 600 yards wide. It strengthened after crossing US-23, tracked through Berlin Township, crossed the Olentangy River, passed near Sunbury and Galena and continued east out of the county. Homes lost siding and roofing, barns were destroyed and transmission towers came down near Olentangy Berlin High School. The Delaware Gazette counted at least 254 homes and businesses affected, 25 with major damage, and the state declared an emergency. The same evening, a tornado from Union County crossed into western Delaware County as an EF1 toward Marysville Road.

It was not the first. The Weather Service confirmed an EF0 near Delaware Lake and Delaware State Park in August 2016, and another EF0 in September 2020 that ran from east of Delaware toward Sunbury and partially lifted one home's roof. Central Ohio as a whole has also lived through the 2008 Hurricane Ike windstorm and the 2012 derecho, with gusts of 70–80+ mph across the region. Wind, hail and the occasional tornado are part of owning a roof in Berlin Township, Sunbury, Galena and every community in between.

First hour

Step 1: Stay Safe Before You Look at the Roof

Damage can wait a few minutes; injuries cannot be undone. Before you assess anything:

  • Stay off the roof. Wet, storm-loosened shingles are slick, and damaged decking can give way.
  • Treat every downed line as live and report it to your electric provider. Keep children and pets away.
  • If a tree or limb is on the house, stay out of the rooms beneath it until it has been checked.
  • If water is coming through a ceiling, shut off power to that area at the panel if it is safe to reach, and poke a small hole in a bulging ceiling spot over a bucket to relieve the water.

First day

Step 2: Document Everything Before Anything Is Moved

Your insurer will want to see the damage as the storm left it. Use your phone — the photos are time-stamped automatically.

  • Walk around the house and photograph every side, including the roof from the ground at a few distances.
  • Photograph shingles, ridge caps, vent covers and gutter pieces on the lawn before you pick them up, then keep them.
  • Check soft metal for hail dents: gutters, downspouts, window wraps, the AC condenser and mailbox. Dents there are strong evidence of hail size.
  • Inside, photograph ceiling stains, wet insulation in the attic and any damaged belongings.
  • Write down the date and time of the storm and what you saw and heard.
  • Keep receipts for tarps, water cleanup and any hotel stay — they may be reimbursable.

Beyond the shingles

While You're Outside: Gutters, Vents and Trees

Roof damage rarely travels alone. Look for gutters pulled away from the fascia, bent downspouts, missing vent caps, cracked skylights and siding with holes or missing panels on the side that faced the wind. Note any trees that lost large limbs or now lean toward the house — a cracked limb hanging over the roof is the next storm's damage. Damaged gutters belong in the same claim as the roof when the same storm caused them, so photograph them now. Whether you live in an open subdivision on former farmland or an older street in Delaware, start your walk on the side of the house that faced the wind.

First 24 hours

Step 3: Stop Further Water Damage

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. If shingles are missing, a limb punctured the deck or water is coming in, get the opening covered. First few days

Step 4: Get a Professional Roof Inspection

Wind damage is often invisible from the ground. Shingles can lift, break their seal strip and settle back down looking normal, and hail bruises rarely show from a driveway. A roof inspection after a storm should cover every slope, the ridge, all flashing and vents, the gutters and the attic, with photos and measurements you can hand to an adjuster.

Our storm damage roof repair inspections are free. We will tell you plainly whether we see storm damage or ordinary wear — we would rather you skip a claim than file one that does not hold up.

On the job

When homeowners in Berlin Township ask us why their roof looked fine from the street after a big wind, the answer usually turns up when we lift a few tabs along the rakes and ridge. The adhesive strips have torn loose, so the shingles are lying in place but no longer bonded. They look normal on a calm day and then peel off in the next moderate wind. That is why we hand-check seals instead of judging a roof by eye after a storm.

Insurance

Step 5: Open the Claim and Know Your Timelines

Call your insurer to report the loss once you have photos and an inspection report. Ohio Administrative Code 3901-1-54 requires insurers to acknowledge a claim within 15 days, to accept or deny within 21 days of receiving proof of loss (or explain why they need more time and update you every 45 days), and to pay within 10 days of accepting an undisputed amount. Ask whether your policy has a separate wind and hail deductible and whether it is a flat amount or a percentage.

Be present when the adjuster inspects, and ask for a copy of their estimate. Our Ohio roof insurance claims guide covers each stage in detail, including what to do when the estimate leaves out items Ohio code requires.

Avoid problems

Step 6: Be Careful Who You Hire

After a well-known storm, out-of-area crews often go door to door. Ohio has no statewide residential roofing license, so a few habits protect you:

  • Ask for proof of liability insurance and workers' compensation before anyone climbs your roof.
  • Do not sign a power of attorney or let a contractor negotiate your claim — the Ohio Department of Insurance advises against both.
  • Do not pay cash or the full amount up front.
  • Know that a contract signed at your door can generally be canceled until midnight of the third business day under Ohio's Home Solicitation Sales Act.
  • Get the permit pulled. Every Delaware County re-roof needs building-department approval — see our roof permit guide.

Repairs

Step 7: Repair or Replace — and Schedule It

Once the claim is settled, decide between targeted roof repair and full roof replacement. Many replacement-cost policies require repairs to be completed within a set window, often 180 days, to release the full payment, so do not let the paperwork sit. If your roof was already an aging builder-grade installation, the storm may simply have finished what time started; our builder-grade roof lifespan guide explains how to tell. Frequently asked

How long do I have to file a roof claim after a storm in Ohio?

Your policy sets the reporting deadline, and it varies by insurer, so check your declarations page and report promptly. Delays make it harder to show the damage came from a specific storm. Once you file, Ohio rules require the insurer to acknowledge the claim within 15 days.

What should I check on a roof patched in spring 2024?

It is worth it. Quick spring patches were often done to stop leaks, not to match or fully seal the surrounding shingles. A follow-up roof inspection can confirm the repair is holding and that nearby shingles were not loosened.

Can hail damage a roof without leaking right away?

Yes. Hail can bruise or knock granules off shingles without breaking them, and the damage shows up as accelerated wear and leaks months later. Dented gutters and downspouts are a good clue to look for.

Should I let the first roofer who knocks inspect my roof?

You can say no. Ask for proof of insurance, a local reference and a written inspection report, and never sign anything on the spot. A door-to-door contract can generally be canceled until midnight of the third business day.

What if the storm damage is on an old roof?

Insurance pays for storm damage, not wear and tear, but a storm can damage an old roof just as easily as a new one. We document both honestly. Call (740) 555-0177 and we'll tell you what we find.
