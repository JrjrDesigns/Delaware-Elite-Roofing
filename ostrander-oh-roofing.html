About Ostrander

A railroad village that's growing fast

Ostrander sits in Scioto Township on the western side of Delaware County. The village was laid out in 1852 alongside the CCC&I Railroad and incorporated on May 18, 1875. Its history shows up around town: a 1925 fire destroyed the business district, the Ostrander Farmers Exchange grain elevator dates to 1948, and the old rail trestle over Blues Creek is a local landmark.

What's changed most recently is size. The 2020 census counted 1,094 residents — up 70.1% from 2010, in a county that was Ohio's fastest-growing by percentage in each of the last three decades. A jump like that generally means new homes alongside the established village, and that mix shapes the roofing work here. Older homes call for a careful, repair-first look; newer homes are usually about documenting the roof while it's young and catching early issues before they turn into leaks.

Either way, the starting point is the same: a free roof inspection with photos of every slope, so you know whether you're looking at a simple roof repair or a full roof replacement before anyone quotes you a price.

On an established village home, that inspection goes beyond the shingles. We look at how the roof meets the chimney and any walls, check the attic for daylight, staining and ventilation, and probe for soft decking near the eaves and valleys, where water tends to sit. On a newer home, we focus on installation details and the condition of the seal strips, and we photograph everything so you have a dated baseline. In both cases you get the same thing at the end: clear photos, a plain-English explanation and a recommendation you can check for yourself — including when the honest answer is that the roof has years left and only needs a small repair.

On the job

In our experience with newer homes in growing villages like Ostrander , the roof problems that show up in the first few years usually come from details, not the shingles themselves — a short piece of flashing where a lower roof meets a wall, a vent that wasn't sealed well, a gutter dumping water right onto a lower roof. None of it is dramatic, and most of it is cheap to fix early. We point those spots out on the first inspection so they get handled while they're still small.

Weather exposure

Storms from the west and open-country wind

Western Delaware County sits in the path of weather moving in from Union County. On March 14, 2024, the National Weather Service tracked an EF2 tornado from Union County that crossed into Delaware County as an EF1 and moved east toward Marysville Road, on a path of about 19.7 miles in total. That same evening a separate EF1 crossed the middle of the county from near US-23 south of Delaware through Berlin Township, with winds up to 110 mph. The NWS survey doesn't place damage in the village itself, but it's a clear reminder that this side of the county gets severe weather too.

Broader central Ohio wind events matter here as well. The June 29, 2012 derecho brought gusts of 70–80+ mph across the region, and the remnants of Hurricane Ike pushed a 75 mph gust through the Columbus airport on September 14, 2008. Homes near open farmland don't get much shelter, and wind can break a shingle's seal strip without removing it — leaving a tab that flaps in every storm afterward.

Then there's winter. Columbus-area January temperatures average 29.6°F, with about 28 inches of snow a year. Melting and refreezing snow at the eaves is why the Residential Code of Ohio requires an ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line. After a storm, use our storm damage checklist, and if your roof is open, call (740) 555-0177 for emergency roof tarping.

Permits

Village and county permits in Ostrander

Ostrander has two permit layers. The village issues its own zoning and building permit, and it states that all permanent, structural items require a Delaware County permit in addition to the Ostrander permit. Delaware County Building Safety is the residential building department for the village, and the Ohio Board of Building Standards treats re-roofing as work that requires plan approval unless it's a minor repair.

What the village hasn't spelled out publicly is whether a straightforward re-roof needs the village permit as well as the county one. Rather than guess, we confirm with the village before the job starts and pull whatever is required. You can read more about how permitting works across the county in our Delaware County re-roof permit guide.

On the job

When homeowners in Ostrander ask us whether they need one permit or two, we tell them honestly that we check every time rather than rely on what we did on the last job. Small villages update their forms and fee schedules, and a roof that involves replacing rotted decking or changing the structure is a different conversation from a like-for-like shingle swap. A quick call before we start costs nothing; a stop-work notice halfway through a tear-off costs a lot.

Frequently asked — Ostrander

Do I need an Ostrander village permit to replace my roof?

The village requires its own permit plus a Delaware County permit for permanent, structural items. Whether a basic re-roof needs the village permit isn't clearly stated, so we confirm with the village before every job and pull whatever is required.

Did the March 2024 tornadoes hit Ostrander?

The NWS tracked an EF2 from Union County into western Delaware County as an EF1, moving east toward Marysville Road. The survey doesn't place damage in the village itself, but if you're unsure about your roof, a free roof inspection will tell you.

My Ostrander home is fairly new. Why would it need a roof inspection?

Newer roofs can have installation details — flashing, vent seals, gutter discharge — that cause problems early. Documenting the roof while it's young also helps with any future storm claim.

How much does a roof replacement cost in Ostrander?
