Housing stock

The Orange Township building boom, twenty years later

Lewis Center is an unincorporated community in northwest Orange Township, with the Olentangy River to the west, Alum Creek State Park to the east and Polaris to the south. US-23 runs through it, Highbanks Metro Park sits along that corridor, and most families here are in Olentangy Local Schools. It is not a census-designated city, so the best way to understand its housing is through Orange Township's own numbers.

They tell a clear story. Orange Township had 3,789 residents in 1990 and 12,464 in 2000, and its 2010 comprehensive plan called it the fastest-growing township in Ohio's fastest-growing county. New-home permits averaged about 405 a year from 1995 onward and peaked at 762 in 2004, with most development between 1991 and 2007. About 78% of the township's housing units are single-family homes, in subdivisions such as River Bend, Walker Woods, Villages of Oak Creek, Wilshire, Abbey Knoll, Glen Oak, North Orange, Olentangy Crossings and McCammon Chase — most of them built between 2000 and 2008.

A house finished at the 2004 peak is now about 22 years old, and the original builder-standard shingles on many of these homes were never meant to last much longer. Our guide on why 1998–2008 builder-grade roofs are failing now covers the signs: granule loss in the gutters, curled tabs on the south and west slopes, and worn valleys. When those show up, a roof replacement is usually the better long-term spend than repeated patching.

On the job

We see a lot of this in Lewis Center subdivisions — once one house on a street gets a new roof, the calls from that street follow. Homes built by the same builder in the same year, with the same shingle line and the same sun exposure, tend to fail on nearly the same timeline. When we inspect one roof on a 2000s cul-de-sac, we can often tell the neighbors what they will find on theirs, and it is usually the south-facing slopes that go first.

Evans Farm & HOAs

Evans Farm and neighborhood design review

The newest piece of Lewis Center is Evans Farm, a New Urbanist community of roughly 1,200 acres north of Lewis Center Road and east of US-23. Ground was broken in 2016 and the first residents arrived in 2018; about 2,200 homes are planned, built by custom builders in bungalow, craftsman, Cape Cod and Victorian styles. Part of it, Parkside at Evans Farm, crosses into Berlin Township.

Evans Farm requires Design Review Committee approval for exterior changes, and older Orange Township subdivisions may have HOAs with their own expectations about shingle color. Our HOA-approved roof replacement service gathers the product, color and specification details a review board asks for so the approval is in hand before materials arrive.

On the job

When Evans Farm homeowners ask us about repairs — the question is usually whether a small fix needs design review at all. Because the community is built around a particular architectural look, we treat anything that changes what you see from the street as something to clear with the committee first, and we document like-for-like repairs with photos so there is no question later. Newer homes also deserve a careful storm inspection, since damage documented early is far easier to resolve than damage found years down the road.

Storms & permits

Storm exposure and who permits your roof

The March 14, 2024 EF1 tornado formed near US-23 south of Delaware and tracked east through Berlin Township, just north of Orange Township, crossing the Olentangy River on its way toward Sunbury and Galena. Lewis Center homes also sit in the path of central Ohio's spring hail and the region's big windstorms, including the June 29, 2012 derecho, when gusts of 70–80+ mph swept the area. After any storm, our storm damage roof repair team inspects and photographs the roof, and the after-a-storm checklist shows you what to document before calling your insurer.

Permits are the other local wrinkle. Unincorporated Orange Township is permitted by Delaware County Building Safety, but parts of the township have been annexed into Columbus or Westerville and use those cities' building departments instead. A Lewis Center mailing address does not settle the question; the parcel does. We check it before we quote and pull the permit from the right office — our Delaware County roof permits guide explains the split. Call (740) 555-0177 if you are not sure which side of the line you are on.

Services

Roofing services for Lewis Center homeowners

Most of our Lewis Center work is on 1990s and 2000s roofs: roof repair for lifted shingles, cracked pipe boots and worn valleys, roof leak repair when a ceiling stain appears, and full replacements when the shingles themselves are done. We also replace gutters and provide roof inspections for home sales and insurance. Every visit starts with a free inspection and photos you can keep.

On a typical 2000s Lewis Center roof, we look at more than the shingles. Plastic pipe boots crack in the sun, step flashing along a two-story sidewall loosens, and many original roofs went on without the ice-and-water membrane in the valleys that a careful replacement includes today. The Residential Code of Ohio now requires an ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line, and a replacement brings your roof up to that standard.

Frequently asked — Lewis Center

Who issues roofing permits for Lewis Center addresses?

For unincorporated Orange Township, Delaware County Building Safety. Some parts of the township have been annexed into Columbus or Westerville, which run their own departments. We look up your parcel and file with the correct office.

My Orange Township home was built around 2004. Should I replace the roof now?

Not automatically, but it is time for a close look. Many original roofs from the 2004 building peak are showing granule loss and worn valleys. A free roof inspection tells you whether a repair will carry you a few more years.

Do I need Evans Farm approval to replace my roof?

Evans Farm requires Design Review Committee approval for exterior changes, so plan on submitting before work starts. We prepare the product and color details the committee will want to review.

Several neighbors are replacing their roofs. Does that mean mine is failing too?

It is a good reason to check. Homes built by the same builder in the same years often age together. Call (740) 555-0177 for a free inspection and an honest answer.

Was Lewis Center in the path of the March 2024 tornado?

The EF1 tracked through Berlin Township, just north of Orange Township. If you saw wind damage nearby that night, an inspection can rule out lifted shingles or loosened flashing — see our storm damage checklist.
