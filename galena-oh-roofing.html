Housing stock

A village from 1816 with newer neighbors

Galena's history goes back more than two centuries. It was platted in 1816 under the name Zoar, and its post office has been called Galena since 1834. The village sits in Berkshire Township, had 924 residents at the 2020 census and an estimated 1,018 in 2023, and lies north of Hoover Reservoir, which flooded the Big Walnut valley when its dam was built in the 1950s.

That gives Galena two very different kinds of roofs. In the historic village core, homes have generations of repairs, additions and reroofs behind them — porches, rear additions and chimneys added at different times, each with its own flashing detail. Around the village, Berkshire Township has filled in with newer subdivisions, including the Northstar community, where production-built roofs are a different conversation. Our roof repair work in the village leans repair-first, while our builder-grade roof lifespan guide is the better starting point for the newer homes nearby.

On the job

When we open up roofs on Galena's older homes — we often find that the shingles you can see are not the only layer. Older houses have frequently been reroofed over the top of an earlier roof, sometimes more than once. The Residential Code of Ohio does not allow a new covering over a roof that already has two layers, so a home that has reached that point needs a full tear-off down to the deck. We check the layer count during the inspection so the estimate reflects it, rather than discovering it on tear-off day.

Storm exposure

The March 14, 2024 tornado and Galena

The National Weather Service tracked the March 14, 2024 EF1 from near US-23 south of Delaware through Berlin Township, across the Olentangy River and near Sunbury and Galena before it crossed into Licking County. Peak winds reached 110 mph along a path roughly 36 miles long and up to 600 yards wide. The Delaware Gazette named the Village of Galena among the jurisdictions affected, alongside the cities of Delaware and Sunbury and several townships, and counted at least 254 homes and businesses affected countywide, 25 of them with major damage.

Tornado damage is not always obvious. Beyond the roofs that lost shingles that night, many more took a beating that shows up later: creased tabs, loosened ridge cap, bent flashing and gutters pulled away from the fascia. If yours has not been looked at by a roofer since, our storm damage roof repair team will inspect it and document anything we find for your insurer. The after-a-storm checklist covers what to photograph first, and for an open roof, call (740) 555-0177 for emergency roof tarping.

A few points from the Ohio Department of Insurance are worth knowing before you file. Wind and hail deductibles can be a flat amount or a percentage of your home's insured value — 2% on a $300,000 dwelling is $6,000 — and replacement-cost policies often require repairs to be finished within a set window, such as 180 days. The department also advises homeowners not to sign a power of attorney over to a contractor and not to pay the full amount up front.

On the job

We see a lot of this along the 2024 track east of the Olentangy — gutters and fascia that took the hit the roof didn't. Wind that gets under a gutter can pry it loose, bend its hangers and crack the fascia board behind it, while the shingles above look untouched. Left alone, that gap lets water run behind the gutter and into the soffit and wall. We inspect the roof edge as carefully as the field of shingles, and we photograph it for the claim.

Permits

Who permits a roof in Galena

Galena does not run its own building department. Residential building permits for the village are issued by Delaware County Building Safety, the same office that handles the townships around it and the City of Sunbury. Under the Residential Code of Ohio, re-roofing generally needs building-department approval unless it is a minor repair, and the code requires an ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line. We file the permit and schedule the inspections as part of every replacement. Our Delaware County roof permits guide covers the process step by step.

That ice-barrier rule exists for a reason. Central Ohio winters hover around freezing — January's 1991–2020 average at the Columbus airport is 29.6°F, with about 28 inches of snow a season — so snow melts and refreezes at the eaves again and again. On older homes with little attic insulation and no ice-and-water membrane, that cycle pushes water up under the bottom courses of shingles. A replacement brings the eaves up to current code, and a repair can often add membrane at the problem spots.

Frequently asked — Galena

Who issues roofing permits for homes in Galena?

Delaware County Building Safety issues residential building permits for Galena; the village does not run its own building department. We file the permit and schedule inspections for you.

My older Galena home already has two layers of shingles. Can you roof over them?

No. The Residential Code of Ohio does not allow a new covering over a roof that already has two layers, so a full tear-off is required. We confirm the layer count during a free roof inspection.

Was the Village of Galena hit by the March 2024 EF1?

Yes. The Delaware Gazette listed the Village of Galena among the jurisdictions hit by the March 14, 2024 EF1. If your roof has not been inspected since, call (740) 555-0177.

The wind pulled my gutters loose but the roof looks fine. Is that covered?

Wind damage to gutters and fascia is often part of a storm claim. We photograph the roof edge and the shingles together so your adjuster sees the full picture. Our storm damage checklist explains what to document.

Do you repair roofs on older homes rather than replacing them?

Whenever a repair makes sense, yes. Flashing, chimney and porch-roof repairs can extend the life of a sound roof, and we will tell you honestly when replacement is the better value.
