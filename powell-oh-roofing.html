Housing stock

A city that more than doubled in twenty years

Powell was settled in 1801, took its current name in 1857, became a village in 1947 and a city in 2000. Since then it has grown fast: 6,247 residents in 2000, 11,500 in 2010 and 14,163 in 2020. The city sits about 14 miles north of Columbus between the Scioto and Olentangy rivers, with its historic Four Corners at SR-750 and Liberty Street and Sawmill Parkway carrying traffic to the newer neighborhoods. The Columbus Zoo and Aquarium is just outside the city in Liberty Township.

Golf Village is a big part of that story: a roughly 1,000-acre golf-course community of about 1,500 units that was annexed into Powell. Development began after 2000 along the Sawmill Parkway extension, built out through the mid-2000s and picked up again in 2012–13, and many of the larger custom homes around the Kinsale course were built by Romanelli & Hughes. The first wave of those roofs is now old enough that roof replacement is a real conversation, and our builder-grade roof lifespan guide explains why 2000s-era shingles are wearing out on a similar schedule across the county.

On the job

We see a lot of this on Powell's larger homes — roofs with many intersecting gables and hips, where the valleys wear out years before the open fields of shingles do. Permits

Powell's re-roof permit: what the city asks for

Powell is one of the few places in Delaware County with its own building department, and its 2025 re-roof handout is direct: all re-roofing work in the City of Powell requires a permit. The submittal includes a Roofing Data Worksheet and a plan view of the roof showing the pitch, hips, valleys, ridges and any chimney. During the job, the city wants PDF photos of the felt and the ice guard at the eaves, the flashing and the valleys. Zoning gives its final, then the building final follows — and it can be approved from the photos.

Two details catch people off guard. First, Powell's handout says the roofing material must be the same as what was previously installed unless structural modifications are made, so switching from asphalt shingles to something like metal roofing takes more planning. Second, the photo requirement means the ice barrier, underlayment and flashing have to be photographed before they are covered — that cannot be reconstructed later. We build the photo set into our install process. Our Delaware County roof permits guide compares Powell's process with the county's.

What the photos show is set by the Residential Code of Ohio. The ice barrier has to run from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line, using two cemented layers of underlayment or a self-adhering membrane, and a new roof cannot be laid over a roof that already has two layers. On most Powell homes that means a full tear-off, new ice and water protection at every eave and in the valleys, new flashing where the roof meets walls and chimneys, and fresh underlayment before a single shingle goes down.

On the job

When Powell homeowners ask what they get at the end of the job — we point to the photo set. Because the city wants the ice guard, valleys and flashing documented before shingles go on, our crews photograph each of those stages anyway. Homeowners tell us that folder turns out to be useful well beyond the permit: it shows a future buyer, an insurer or a home inspector exactly what is under the shingles.

Weather

Hail, wind and the Powell storm season

Central Ohio's severe storms bring large hail and occasional tornadoes, especially in spring, and public storm-report compilations show dozens of hail reports within about ten miles of Powell since 2004, with spring the busiest stretch. Powell homes also rode out the region's major windstorms — the September 2008 remnants of Hurricane Ike and the June 29, 2012 derecho, when gusts of 70–80+ mph swept central Ohio.

Hail damage is easy to miss from the ground. Bruised shingles and dented vents may not leak for a season or two, and replacement-cost policies often set a deadline to complete repairs. After a hailstorm, our storm damage roof repair team inspects, photographs and measures the roof, and we can meet your adjuster on site. Call (740) 555-0177 after any storm that leaves hail on the lawn.

Ohio's claim-handling rule, OAC 3901-1-54, requires insurers to acknowledge a claim within 15 days and to accept or deny it within 21 days of receiving proof of loss, or explain why they need more time. The Ohio Department of Insurance also warns homeowners not to sign over power of attorney to a contractor or pay the full amount up front. Frequently asked — Powell

Does every roof replacement in Powell need a permit?

Yes. Powell's 2025 re-roof handout says all re-roofing work in the city requires a permit. We prepare the Roofing Data Worksheet and roof plan and submit the inspection photos for you.

Why does Powell want photos of my roof during installation?

The city reviews photos of the felt, the ice guard at the eaves, the flashing and the valleys, and can approve the building final from them. Those layers are covered by shingles, so they have to be photographed as the work happens.

Can I switch from shingles to metal on my Powell home?

Powell's handout says material must match what was previously installed unless structural modifications are made, so a material change needs extra review. Call (740) 555-0177 and we will talk through what the city is likely to require.

Are mid-2000s Golf Village roofs due for replacement?

Many original roofs from that era are showing their age, but not every one needs replacing. A free roof inspection looks at granule loss, valleys and seals and tells you honestly whether a repair will do.

Do you handle Golf Village Design Review Board approval?

We put together the shingle color and product information design review boards typically ask for and help you submit it before materials are ordered. See our HOA roof replacement page for details.
