The extra step

Why HOA approval comes before your new roof

Delaware County's growth years produced dozens of planned communities, many built around golf courses and governed by homeowners associations with design review boards. Those boards exist to keep a neighborhood's look consistent, and the roof – the biggest visible surface on the house – is one of the first things they care about. Replace it with the wrong color or material, or without approval, and you can face a violation notice, fines, or a demand to change it.

The timing makes this a big deal right now. Scioto Reserve in Concord Township began development in 1998 and Tartan Fields in 1997. Golf Village in Powell began building out after 2000. Highland Lakes in Genoa Township was built mostly in the 1990s and early 2000s. Those first-generation roofs are now 20 to 29 years old, and entire streets are replacing them within a few years of each other. Design review boards are seeing a wave of roof applications, and an incomplete submittal goes to the back of the line.

We handle the submittal as part of the job. Call (740) 555-0177 for a free roof inspection and we will tell you what your association is likely to require.

Scioto Reserve

Scioto Reserve: the Driftwood / Weathered Wood standard

Scioto Reserve is the clearest example in the county of how HOA roof rules work. The community spans about 695 acres with more than 1,100 single-family homes plus condominiums, and its rules are specific. The Scioto Reserve HOA's Design Review Board page states that all roof replacements must be applied for, with approval received prior to installation, and that shingle color is reviewed case by case. The Master Association rules add that any change in shingle color must be approved by the Design Review Board, and they identify the community's original roof color as Driftwood / Weathered Wood.

There is a wrinkle. Older versions of the written rules suggest a same-color replacement does not need approval, while the board's own page says all replacements do. Our advice is simple: submit anyway. An approval you did not strictly need costs nothing; a violation on a brand-new roof costs a lot.

Scioto Reserve's rules also address solar: panels are limited to non-street-facing and rear elevations and require Design Review Board approval. If you are planning solar now or later, we coordinate the roof layout and approval so the two projects do not conflict.

On the job

When homeowners in Scioto Reserve ask us whether they really need to apply if they are keeping the same color, we point them to the board's own wording – all roof replacements – and offer to prepare the application either way. Most people are relieved. The worst-case scenario is not a slow approval; it is a neighbor's complaint after the roof is on, followed by a letter from the board asking for documentation the homeowner never filed.

What we submit

What goes into an approval packet

Every association has its own form, but most design review boards want the same core information. We assemble it, fill out the application with you, and submit it on your behalf or hand you a ready-to-sign packet.

  • Completed association application with property address and lot information
  • Manufacturer, product line and exact color name for the proposed shingle
  • Physical shingle samples or manufacturer color sheets, plus photos of the current roof
  • Notes on any change in profile or material, such as architectural to designer shingles or a metal accent roof
  • Ridge, vent and trim colors, and gutter color if gutters are being replaced
  • Contractor information, proof of insurance and the projected schedule
  • Any solar, skylight or ventilation changes that alter the roof's appearance
On the job

We see a lot of this in Tartan Fields and the other late-1990s golf communities: two neighbors both install a shingle labeled 'Weathered Wood,' and the roofs look noticeably different side by side. Every manufacturer blends its own version of that color, and the original builder-grade shingle has faded for 25-plus years. That is why we include the manufacturer and product line in the packet, bring physical samples to compare against neighboring roofs in daylight, and make sure the board approves the exact product you are getting – not just a color name.

Other communities

Design review across Delaware County

Scioto Reserve is not alone. Golf Village in Powell has a Design Review Board. Evans Farm in Lewis Center requires Design Review Committee approval for exterior changes. Highland Lakes in Genoa Township has active homeowners associations, and newer subdivisions in Berlin Township such as Berlin Manor are HOA communities as well. The specific roof rules vary from community to community, and they change over time, so we always read the current documents or contact the association before we specify a product.

If your association has no published roof standard, we still recommend asking before you sign a contract. A quick email to the management company confirming that a like-for-like replacement needs no review is worth having in writing.

Permits too

HOA approval is not a building permit

Design review approval and a building permit are separate. Ohio's Board of Building Standards requires approval for re-roofing work beyond minor repair, and the permit comes from whichever office covers your address: Delaware County Building Safety for townships including Concord, Genoa, Orange, Liberty and Berlin; or the City of Delaware, Powell or Westerville inside those cities. Concord Township itself does not require a township zoning permit for roof replacement, but the county building permit still applies. Powell requires a permit for every re-roof, with photos of the underlayment, ice guard, flashing and valleys, and its handout says the material must match what was previously installed unless structural changes are made – which matters if your HOA approves a material change. Frequently asked

Do I need HOA approval to replace my roof with the same color?

In some communities, yes. Scioto Reserve's Design Review Board states that all roof replacements must be approved before installation. Even where a same-color replacement is technically exempt, we recommend submitting so you have approval on file.

What is the Driftwood / Weathered Wood standard?

It is the original community roof color identified in Scioto Reserve's master association rules. Any change in shingle color there must be approved by the Design Review Board. We match that color family with current products and include samples in the packet.

How long does HOA roof approval take?

It depends on the association's meeting schedule and how complete the application is. A complete packet with product details and samples avoids the most common delay – a request for more information. We submit as early as possible and apply for the permit in parallel.

Can I switch to a metal roof in my HOA neighborhood?

Possibly, but a material change is a bigger ask than a color match and will get closer review. In Powell, the city's re-roof rules also address material changes. See our metal roofing page and call (740) 555-0177 to talk it through.

Does my HOA approval replace the building permit?

No. HOA approval and the building permit are separate. We obtain both: the design review approval from your association and the permit from Delaware County Building Safety, the City of Delaware, Powell or Westerville.

Can I add solar panels when I replace my roof in Scioto Reserve?

Scioto Reserve's rules allow solar on non-street and rear elevations with Design Review Board approval. Coordinating the roof and solar submittals together keeps the two projects from conflicting.
