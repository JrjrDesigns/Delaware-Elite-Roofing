Housing stock

Historic homes, carriage houses and newer edges of town

Delaware is the county seat, founded in 1808 and home to 41,302 people as of 2020, about 30 miles north of Columbus where US-23, US-36, US-42 and SR-37 meet. Ohio Wesleyan University, founded in 1842, anchors the center of town, and the Delaware County Fairgrounds hosts the Little Brown Jug. That long history shows up on the rooftops. The Historic Northwest Neighborhood alone has more than 500 homes and carriage houses listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Older houses like these tend to have steeper pitches, chimneys that have been repointed more than once, and secondary roofs over porches, bays and outbuildings. Those transitions are where most of our roof leak repair calls in the older parts of town begin. On the edges of the city, newer subdivisions — some of them technically in neighboring townships with a Delaware mailing address — bring a different problem: original builder shingles reaching the end of their life. If that sounds like your house, read our guide on builder-grade roof lifespan before you decide anything.

On the job

In our experience with Delaware's older neighborhoods — the carriage house is the roof nobody is watching. Homeowners keep the main house in good shape, but the building out back gets looked at only when something is visibly wrong. By the time we are called, a small leak at a ridge or a rotted fascia board has often been working on the framing for a few seasons. When we inspect a historic lot, we look at every roof on the property, not just the one facing the street.

Storm exposure

Three tornadoes in or near Delaware since 2016

National Weather Service surveys document three tornadoes in or near the city in less than a decade. On August 20, 2016, an EF0 with 75 mph winds touched down near Delaware Lake, Buckeye Valley Middle School, US-23 and the Delaware Dam, causing roof damage from fallen trees and a partial barn roof collapse. On September 7, 2020, an EF0 began about four miles east of the city and partially lifted the roof of a home, with 60–65 mph straight-line winds reported in the Delaware area.

Then on March 14, 2024, an EF1 formed near US-23 just south of Delaware and strengthened as it moved east through Berlin Township toward Sunbury and Galena. The Delaware Gazette listed the City of Delaware among the jurisdictions affected. If your home took a hit — or a tree came down on your roof — our storm damage roof repair team documents everything for your insurer, and our roof insurance claims guide covers the deadlines Ohio insurers must meet. For an open roof tonight, call (740) 555-0177 for emergency roof tarping.

Tornadoes make headlines, but ordinary spring thunderstorms also bring wind and hail across central Ohio. Mature trees near older homes add their own risk: a falling limb can puncture a roof deck in seconds, and smaller branches scrape granules off shingles for years before anyone notices. Keeping limbs trimmed back from the roofline is one of the cheapest roof protections there is.

Permits & historic review

City permits and the Downtown Historic District

Unlike most of the county, the City of Delaware runs its own building department, and roof permits are filed through the city's online portal. The city also stopped requiring contractor registration starting in 2026, so registration status is no longer a useful way to vet a roofer — ask for insurance certificates and a written contract instead.

If your property is in the Downtown Historic District, the city says you must apply for a Certificate of Appropriateness before you begin work on a roof or any other exterior alteration. Roofs are often approved administratively, while larger projects go before the Historic Preservation Commission. We prepare the COA application alongside the building permit so neither one holds up the other. National Register listing in the Northwest Neighborhood is a separate matter from the downtown district, and we confirm with the city which review, if any, applies to your address. Our Delaware County permit guide explains how the city's process differs from the county's.

On the job

When downtown owners ask us how long the historic approval takes — the honest answer is that the paperwork rarely slows a roof down if it is started first. Where projects stall is when someone orders materials, schedules a crew and only then learns a Certificate of Appropriateness was needed. We put the COA at the top of the checklist, ahead of the material order, every time.

Services

Roofing services for Delaware homeowners

Most calls we get in Delaware fall into one of a few groups: storm damage, leaks in older homes, and full replacements on houses that have simply reached the end of their roof. We handle all three, plus gutters and full inspections.

Historic and older homes call for a repair-first approach. A well-flashed chimney or a rebuilt porch roof can add years to a main roof that is otherwise sound, and we would rather tell you that than sell a replacement you don't need. When replacement is the right call, the Residential Code of Ohio does not allow a new layer over a roof that already has two, and an ice barrier is required from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line. Frequently asked — Delaware

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in the City of Delaware?

Yes — the city directs roof permits to its online portal form. We file it for you as part of the job and schedule the inspections.

My house is in downtown Delaware. Do I need historic approval for a new roof?

Inside the Downtown Historic District, the city requires a Certificate of Appropriateness before roof work begins. Roof projects are often approved administratively. We prepare the application with the permit — call (740) 555-0177 to start.

Does a National Register listing in the Northwest Neighborhood restrict my roofing choices?

National Register listing is separate from the city's downtown design review. We check with the city about your specific address before you choose materials, so there are no surprises.

Can you repair the roof on my detached carriage house or garage?

Yes. We inspect and repair secondary roofs on historic lots, including carriage houses, porches and garages. See our roof repair page for what that typically includes.

A tree fell on my roof in Delaware during a storm. What should I do first?

Get everyone out of the affected rooms, photograph the damage from a safe spot, and call (740) 555-0177 for emergency tarping. Our storm damage checklist lists what your insurer will want to see.
