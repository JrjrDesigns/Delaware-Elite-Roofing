Delaware County Roof Permits: Who Issues Yours and What Else You Need

Delaware County has several building departments, a downtown historic district and several HOA design boards. Here is which office handles your re-roof and what approvals come first.

The rule

Yes, a Delaware County Re-Roof Needs a Permit

Ohio's Board of Building Standards addressed re-roofing of one-, two- and three-family homes directly in a 2017 memo: plan approval is required for all reroofing projects unless the work is considered a minor repair. That rule applies wherever a certified residential building department has jurisdiction, and every part of Delaware County is covered by one. Delaware County's own building code requires a permit to alter, repair or replace, and roofing does not appear on the county's list of work exempt from approval.

In practice, that means a full tear-off and re-roof in Delaware County should always go through a building department. Small patch repairs are a different matter, which is why we tell you up front whether your roof repair needs a permit or not. When we do a roof replacement, the permit is part of our job and part of the price.

By community

What That Looks Like Community by Community

Townships. Homeowners in Liberty Township, Concord Township, Berlin Township, Genoa Township and unincorporated Orange Township around Lewis Center get their building permit from Delaware County Building Safety. Townships handle zoning, not building permits. Concord Township states that roof repair or replacement does not need a township zoning permit, and Genoa Township's own residential improvement permit covers additions, decks and fences, not roofs.

City of Delaware. Roof permits are filed through the city's online portal with the Delaware City Building Department. As of 2026 the city no longer requires contractors to register, but the permit itself is still required.

Powell. The City of Powell states that all re-roofing work requires a permit. Its 2025 re-roof handout asks for a roofing data worksheet and a plan view showing pitch, hips, valleys, ridges and chimneys, then photo documentation of the felt and ice guard at the eaves, flashing and valleys. Inspections run zoning final, then building final, and can be approved from photos. Powell also requires the new roofing material to be the same as what was previously installed unless structural modifications are made, so switching from shingles to metal on a Powell home means extra review.

Villages and Sunbury. Sunbury, Galena, Ashley, Ostrander and Shawnee Hills residential permits come from Delaware County Building Safety. Ostrander also issues its own village permits and says permanent structural items need a county permit in addition to the village permit; whether a simple re-roof needs the village permit is something we confirm with the village for each job. Sunbury has a city zoning review process, which we check on Sunbury jobs before filing.

On the job

In our experience with Powell re-roofs, the step that catches people off guard is the photo documentation. The city wants to see the ice guard at the eaves, the underlayment and the valley and flashing work — all of which disappear under shingles within hours. A crew that does not plan for it ends up peeling shingles back to prove what is underneath. We photograph each stage as it goes down, so the final can be approved from pictures without anyone pulling the roof apart.

Historic review

Downtown Delaware Historic District: Certificate of Appropriateness

If your home or building is in the City of Delaware's Downtown Historic District, a permit is not the first step. The city states that you must apply for a Certificate of Appropriateness (COA) before you begin work on a roof or other exterior alteration. The good news is that roofs are often approved administratively by staff; larger projects go before the Historic Preservation Commission.

The Historic Northwest Neighborhood, with more than 500 homes and carriage houses listed on the National Register, is a separate matter. National Register listing by itself does not generally trigger local design review, but rules can change and boundaries matter, so we confirm with the city's planning staff before starting any City of Delaware roof near downtown. On older homes, careful roof leak repair at chimneys and wall flashing is often the right first step.

HOA approval

HOA Design Review Comes Before the Permit

Building departments check that a roof is built to code. Homeowners associations check how it looks, and in HOA communities you need both. The clearest example is Scioto Reserve in Concord Township. Its HOA states that all roof replacements must be applied for and approved before installation, its master association rules say any change in shingle color must be approved by the Design Review Board, and the original community color is Driftwood/Weathered Wood. Its 2022 rules suggest a same-color replacement may not need approval, but the HOA's current Design Review Board page says all replacements do, so we always submit.

Evans Farm requires Design Review Committee approval for exterior changes, Golf Village in Powell has a Design Review Board, and Highland Lakes in the Genoa Township and Westerville area has active HOAs. Each association sets its own process. Our HOA-approved roof replacement service prepares color sheets, product specifications and the application so approval is in hand before the permit is filed.

Code basics

What the Building Department Is Checking

Residential roofing in Ohio is governed by Chapter 9 of the Residential Code of Ohio, and the same core rules apply whichever office issues your permit. Two matter on almost every Delaware County re-roof:

  • Layer limit. A new roof cannot go over an existing roof that already has two or more layers of covering; everything has to be torn off to the deck first.
  • Ice barrier. An ice barrier is required from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line, using self-adhering polymer-modified membrane or two cemented layers of underlayment.
  • Flashing and underlayment. Inspectors look at valleys, wall and chimney flashing and underlayment coverage — which is exactly why Powell asks for photos of them.
  • Damaged decking. Rotted or delaminated sheathing found during tear-off should be replaced before new roofing goes on, a common finding on original roofs covered in our builder-grade roof lifespan guide.

Bottom line

Let the Roofer Pull the Permit

The permit should be in the contractor's name, because the contractor is responsible for building to code and passing inspection. Be wary of anyone who asks you to pull it yourself or suggests skipping it. An unpermitted re-roof can complicate an insurance claim or a home sale later. Frequently asked

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in a Delaware County township?

Yes. Townships do not issue building permits; Delaware County Building Safety does, and Ohio treats re-roofing as requiring approval unless it is a minor repair. We pull the permit as part of every roof replacement.

How long does a Delaware County roof permit take?

It varies by office and season. In our experience, the permit itself is rarely what holds up a job — HOA design review or a historic district COA usually takes longer, so we start those first.

My address says Powell, but I'm not sure I live in the city. Which rules apply?

Many homes with a Powell mailing address are actually in Liberty or Concord Township, which use the county permit office. Powell's photo-documented permit applies only inside city limits. We check the parcel record before filing.

Does the Northwest Neighborhood in Delaware need historic approval for a new roof?

National Register listing alone does not usually require local design review, but the Downtown Historic District does require a Certificate of Appropriateness. We confirm with city planning staff for any home near downtown.

Can my HOA reject the shingle color I want?

Yes, if its rules give the design board authority over color, as Scioto Reserve's do. Submitting manufacturer color samples with the application avoids most problems. Call (740) 555-0177 and we'll prepare it.
