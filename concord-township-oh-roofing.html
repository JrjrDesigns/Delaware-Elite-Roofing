Housing stock

Golf-course communities from the late 1990s, now on their first roofs

Concord Township sits on the west side of Delaware County along the Scioto River and O'Shaughnessy Reservoir, whose dam dates to 1925. US-42, SR-257 and SR-745 carry most of its traffic, and the township includes Bellepoint along with portions of Delaware, Dublin and Shawnee Hills. The 2020 census counted 11,207 residents, and the township's 2026 comprehensive plan draft counts roughly 5,800 residential units, 4,019 of them single-family homes.

Two neighborhoods define much of that housing. Scioto Reserve covers 695 acres; development started in 1998, and the plan counts 1,163 single-family homes and 92 condominiums. Its golf club, at 7383 Scioto Parkway with a Powell mailing address, opened in 2001 and is surrounded by roughly 1,400 homes. Tartan Fields, on 445 acres, started in 1997 and has 598 homes. Later phases followed — Scioto Reserve North in 2004, The Oaks in 2005 — and newer neighborhoods like Clarkshaw Village and Hollybrook arrived in 2024.

The first generation of roofs in Scioto Reserve and Tartan Fields is now more than 25 years old. On larger homes with complex rooflines, age shows first in a few places: valleys where two roof planes funnel water together, step flashing along sidewalls and dormers, and the ridge and hip caps that take the most sun. Our guide to why 1998–2008 subdivision roofs are failing now covers the warning signs.

On the job

In our experience with Scioto Reserve and Tartan Fields homes , the valleys tell the story first. On a big, cut-up roofline, every valley carries the water from two whole slopes, and after 25-plus years the shingles there are often worn thinner than anywhere else on the house. We'll frequently find a roof where the open field of shingles still has some life but the valleys are near failure — and that's the detail that decides whether a repair is worth doing or whether it's time for the full replacement.

Scioto Reserve HOA

Scioto Reserve Design Review Board approval and the Driftwood/Weathered Wood standard

If you live in Scioto Reserve, your roof replacement starts with paperwork. The Scioto Reserve HOA's Design Review Board page states that all roof replacements must be applied for, with approval received prior to installation, and that shingle color is reviewed case by case. The Master Association rules add that any change in shingle color must be approved by the Design Review Board, and they identify the community's original color as Driftwood/Weathered Wood.

There's a wrinkle: the 2022 rules suggest a same-color replacement doesn't need approval, while the Design Review Board page says every replacement does. Our advice is simple — submit anyway. An approval letter costs you a little lead time; skipping it can cost you a lot more if the association disagrees after the new roof is on. The same rules also address solar panels, which are limited to non-street or rear elevations and require Design Review Board approval.

We prepare the submittal for you: manufacturer, product line, color name and sample photos, matched to the Driftwood/Weathered Wood standard or to the alternate color you want reviewed. That's the core of our HOA-approved roof replacement service, and it means the board sees a complete packet the first time. Questions about your specific street or phase? Call (740) 555-0177.

On the job

When Scioto Reserve homeowners ask us whether they can switch away from the original color, we tell them it's possible to ask — the board reviews color case by case — but to plan for the review time and have a backup choice in the Driftwood/Weathered Wood family. We bring physical samples so they can see both options against their brick and trim in daylight before the packet goes in, which tends to make the decision easier for everyone.

Permits & weather

Permits and weather exposure in Concord Township

Concord Township's zoning office lists roof repair and replacement as work that does not need a township zoning permit. That doesn't mean no permit at all — residential building permits and inspections come from Delaware County Building Safety, and the Ohio Board of Building Standards treats re-roofing as work that needs plan approval unless it's a minor repair. Homes in the parts of the township inside Delaware or Dublin answer to those cities instead. Our Delaware County roof permit guide explains how it breaks down, and we pull the permit as part of every job.

On the weather side, homes along O'Shaughnessy Reservoir, the Scioto River and the open fairways take more direct wind than homes tucked into wooded streets. Central Ohio has seen gusts of 70–80+ mph in the June 29, 2012 derecho, and in March 2024 an EF2 tornado from Union County crossed into western Delaware County as an EF1. After any significant storm, run through our storm damage checklist and call us for an inspection — if your roof is open, our emergency tarping service is available around the clock.

Frequently asked — Concord Township

Do I need Scioto Reserve Design Review Board approval to replace my roof with the same color?

The HOA's Design Review Board page says all roof replacements must be applied for and approved before installation, while the 2022 rules suggest a same-color replacement may not need approval. We recommend submitting either way, and we prepare the packet for you.

What is the standard shingle color in Scioto Reserve?

The Master Association rules identify the original community color as Driftwood/Weathered Wood, and any change in shingle color must be approved by the Design Review Board. See our HOA roof replacement process.

Does Concord Township require a zoning permit for a new roof?

No — the township lists roof repair and replacement as not needing a township zoning permit. The building permit comes from Delaware County Building Safety, and we pull it for you.

How old are the roofs in Tartan Fields and Scioto Reserve?

Tartan Fields started in 1997 and Scioto Reserve in 1998, so original roofs there are now 25+ years old. A free roof inspection shows whether yours needs repair or replacement — call (740) 555-0177.

Can I put solar panels on my Scioto Reserve roof?

The Scioto Reserve rules limit solar panels to non-street or rear elevations and require Design Review Board approval. If you're planning solar, it's worth replacing an aging roof first.
