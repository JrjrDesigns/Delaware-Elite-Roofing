Why now

The Delaware County build-out is coming due

Delaware County was the fastest-growing county in Ohio by percentage in the 1990s, the 2000s and again from 2010 to 2020, climbing from about 67,000 residents in 1990 to more than 214,000 in 2020. Almost all of that growth arrived as new subdivisions in the southern townships – and almost every one of those houses went up with the same kind of roof: a builder-grade asphalt shingle chosen to keep the sale price competitive.

The numbers tell the story. Genoa Township permitted 3,810 new homes between 1998 and 2003, peaking at 716 in 2002. Orange Township averaged roughly 405 housing permits a year from 1995 on and hit 762 in 2004. Scioto Reserve in Concord Township broke ground in 1998 and Tartan Fields in 1997. Builder-grade shingles from that era commonly wear out somewhere in their twenties, which is exactly where a huge share of Genoa Township, Lewis Center and Orange Township and Concord Township roofs sit today.

If your house was built during that wave, a replacement is not a question of if – it is a question of whether you plan it on your own schedule or have it forced on you by a spring storm. A free roof inspection tells you which side of that line you are on. Call (740) 555-0177 and we will come look before the next storm season.

On the job

We see a lot of this in Genoa Township – on houses from the 1998–2003 boom, the south- and west-facing slopes are usually far worse than the north side. The sun-facing shingles have shed their granules and gone brittle at the tabs while the north slope still looks almost respectable from the driveway. Homeowners glance up at the front of the house, see a decent roof, and have no idea the back slope over the family room is the one about to start leaking.

Exposure

Open farmland, young trees and a lot of wind

Much of southern Delaware County was cropland a generation ago, and many unincorporated areas were zoned for one- to five-acre lots. In Liberty Township, about 71 percent of the land is zoned FR-1 farm residence with a one-acre minimum. That means wide-open sight lines across former fields and street trees that are still too young to break the wind. A roof in a 2004 subdivision off Sawmill Parkway or US-23 takes the full force of every west wind that comes across the fields.

That exposure is not theoretical. On March 14, 2024, an EF1 tornado with peak winds of 110 mph tracked roughly 36 miles across the county – forming near US-23 south of Delaware, crossing Berlin Township and the Olentangy River, and passing near Sunbury and Galena. The Delaware Gazette reported at least 254 homes and businesses affected. EF0 tornadoes also touched down near Delaware Lake in 2016 and east of Delaware in 2020. When we spec a new roof here, we spec it for wind: six-nail installation, sealed starter strips at every eave and rake, and wind-rated hip and ridge caps.

What we install

What a Delaware Elite Roofing replacement includes

Every replacement starts with a full tear-off down to the decking. Ohio's Residential Code does not allow a new covering over a roof that already has two or more layers, and we do not recommend going over even a single layer – you cannot inspect or repair decking you cannot see.

  • Full tear-off and disposal, with magnetic sweeps of the yard and driveway
  • Decking inspection; soft or delaminated sheets replaced and documented with photos
  • Ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line, as the Residential Code of Ohio requires, plus valleys and around penetrations
  • Synthetic underlayment over the rest of the deck
  • New drip edge, step and counter flashing, pipe boots and ventilation matched to your attic
  • Architectural shingles installed to the manufacturer's high-wind pattern; impact-resistant Class 4 shingles available
  • Permit pulled from the correct office and final inspection scheduled
  • HOA color and material approval packet prepared when your community requires it

Paperwork

Four permit offices, one contractor

Ohio's Board of Building Standards requires plan approval for re-roofing a one-, two- or three-family home unless the job is a minor repair, and every part of Delaware County is covered by a certified building department. The catch is figuring out which one. Every township – Orange, Genoa, Berlin, Liberty, Concord and the rest – along with Sunbury, Galena, Ashley and Ostrander, is permitted by Delaware County Building Safety. The City of Delaware issues its own roof permits through an online portal. Powell requires a permit for every re-roof and asks for photos of the felt, ice guard at the eaves, flashing and valleys. Homes in the annexed parts of Genoa and Orange townships with Westerville addresses go through the City of Westerville.

Mailing addresses do not settle it. A Liberty Township house can carry a Powell or Delaware address, and part of Orange Township sits inside Columbus. We confirm the jurisdiction from the parcel before we quote, so the permit, the inspection and the final all land in the right place. Our Delaware County roof permit guide walks through each office in detail.

On the job

In our experience with Liberty Township homes on one-acre lots, the first thing we check after tear-off is the decking along the upper third of the roof. Production builders of that era often relied on minimal ridge and soffit venting, and on a big, unshaded roof the attic runs hot all summer. We regularly find sheathing near the ridge that has darkened and gone soft while the lower courses are fine – something nobody could have seen from the ground and the reason we never quote a roof as 'no decking needed' before the old shingles come off.

Budget

What a roof replacement costs here

Central Ohio asphalt re-roofs typically land between about $8,000 and $18,000, with architectural shingles running roughly $4.50 to $6 per square foot installed and impact-resistant Class 4 shingles about $5 to $7 or more. Larger two-story homes with steep pitches, multiple valleys and dormers – common in the bigger Delaware County subdivisions – trend toward the top of that range. Tear-off of an extra layer adds cost, as does any decking replacement.

Those are planning numbers, not a quote. Your real number comes from a free on-roof inspection and measurement. For a deeper breakdown, see our roof replacement cost guide for Delaware County, or call (740) 555-0177 to schedule the measurement.

Frequently asked

My house in Orange Township was built in 2004. Is it time to replace the roof?

Maybe. A 2004 builder-grade roof is in the age range where most start to fail, but sun exposure, ventilation and storm history matter more than the calendar. Our free inspection checks granule loss, seal strips, flashing and decking soft spots and gives you a straight answer. Call (740) 555-0177 to book it.

Can you put new shingles over my old roof to save money?

Ohio's Residential Code prohibits a new covering over a roof that already has two or more layers, and even with one layer we recommend a full tear-off so the decking and flashing can be inspected and replaced. Most Delaware County subdivision homes still have their original single layer, so tear-off is straightforward.

Who pulls the permit for a re-roof in Delaware County?

We do. Townships and the villages are permitted by Delaware County Building Safety; the City of Delaware, Powell and Westerville each run their own building departments. See our Delaware County roof permit guide for the details.

How long will my Delaware County re-roof take from signing to finished?

Most single-family homes are torn off and re-roofed in one to two days once materials are delivered. Permit approval, HOA review and weather can add lead time before work starts, so we build that into the schedule up front.

Should I upgrade to impact-resistant shingles?

In a county that has seen tornadoes in 2016, 2020 and 2024, many homeowners choose Class 4 shingles for added hail resistance; it is worth asking your insurance agent whether your policy treats them differently. They typically cost somewhat more per square foot than standard architectural shingles.

Do I need HOA approval before replacing my roof?

Often, yes. Scioto Reserve, for example, requires roof replacements to be approved by its Design Review Board before installation, and many other communities review shingle color. We prepare the approval packet as part of our HOA roof replacement service.
