The storm record

Delaware County's verified storm history

On the night of March 14, 2024, the National Weather Service confirmed an EF1 tornado with peak winds of 110 mph that formed near US-23 south of Delaware, strengthened after crossing the highway, and tracked roughly 36 miles – through Berlin Township, across the Olentangy River, and on near Sunbury and Galena. The path was up to 600 yards wide. The NWS documented homes with siding and roof damage, destroyed barns, transmission towers toppled near Olentangy Berlin High School, and in some neighborhoods winds of 95–105 mph with windows blown out and partial roof removal. The Delaware Gazette reported at least 254 homes and businesses affected, 25 with major damage, and a state emergency was declared.

That same night, an EF2 tornado that began in Union County entered western Delaware County as an EF1 and moved east to Marysville Road. Earlier, an EF0 with 80 mph winds tracked from east of Delaware toward Sunbury on September 7, 2020, partially lifting one home's roof and scattering shingles, and another EF0 struck near Delaware Lake, the Delaware Dam and US-23 on August 20, 2016, causing roof damage from fallen trees and a barn roof collapse. Central Ohio as a whole also took the June 2012 derecho, with gusts of 70–80+ mph across the region, and the September 2008 remnants of Hurricane Ike.

Add the ordinary spring hail and thunderstorm season – central Ohio's severe storms bring large hail and, on occasion, tornadoes, especially in spring – and a lot of Delaware County roofs carry storm damage their owners have never seen.

Geography makes it worse here than in older, tree-lined suburbs. The subdivisions in Lewis Center and Orange Township, Genoa Township and Liberty Township were built on open farmland, and their street trees are still young. Homes along Hoover, Alum Creek and O'Shaughnessy reservoirs face long stretches of open water. With nothing to break the wind, a storm reaches these roofs at full strength.

What damage looks like

Wind and hail damage you can't see from the yard

Wind damage is not just missing shingles. The more common problem is a shingle whose adhesive seal strip has broken loose. It lies back down after the storm and looks fine from the ground, but it now flaps in every wind and eventually creases, cracks or tears off. Hail leaves bruises – soft spots where granules have been knocked away and the mat beneath is fractured – along with dents in gutters, vents and downspouts that help confirm the size and direction of the storm.

  • Creased shingle tabs and broken seal strips, especially along rakes, ridges and roof corners
  • Missing or cracked hip and ridge caps
  • Granule loss and circular bruising consistent with hail
  • Dented gutters, downspouts, box vents and metal flashing
  • Lifted or bent drip edge and step flashing
  • Punctures and broken decking from fallen limbs

Insurance

How we help with your insurance claim

We are a roofing contractor, not a public adjuster – in Ohio, nobody may act as a public adjuster without a certificate of authority from the Superintendent of Insurance. What we do is give you and your adjuster clear, honest evidence: dated photos of every damaged area, measurements, and a written scope of repair. We will meet the adjuster on your roof if you want us there.

Ohio Administrative Code 3901-1-54 sets the insurer's clock. Your insurer must acknowledge your claim within 15 days, accept or deny it within 21 days of receiving proof of loss – or tell you it needs more time and update you every 45 days – and pay within 10 days of accepting an undisputed amount. Replacement-cost policies often require repairs to be finished within a set period, such as 180 days, to collect the full replacement value, so read your policy before you wait.

A few ground rules from us. We never waive, rebate or absorb your deductible – your deductible is your contractual share under the policy, and we quote honestly around it. The Ohio Department of Insurance advises homeowners not to give any contractor power of attorney over their claim, not to pay the full amount up front, and to be present for inspections. We agree with all of it. And if you sign a contract with a door-to-door salesperson, Ohio's Home Solicitation Sales Act gives you until midnight of the third business day to cancel.

Our Ohio roof insurance claim guide goes through the full process step by step. Call (740) 555-0177 to schedule a free storm inspection before you call your insurer, so you know what you are filing for.

Frequently asked

Should I call my insurance company or a roofer first?

Many homeowners have a roofer inspect first so they know whether there is real damage before opening a claim. Either order works; just do not let the policy's reporting deadline slip. Call (740) 555-0177 for a free storm inspection.

Will you cover or waive my deductible?

No. We never waive, rebate or absorb deductibles. Your deductible is your share under the policy, and wind/hail deductibles can be a flat amount or a percentage of dwelling coverage – 2% on a $300,000 home is $6,000 – so check yours before you file.

What deadlines does my insurer face once I file a storm claim?

Under OAC 3901-1-54, insurers must acknowledge a claim within 15 days, accept or deny within 21 days of proof of loss (or explain why they need more time, with updates every 45 days), and pay within 10 days of accepting an undisputed amount.

Can you fix a rushed 2024 tornado patch that left damage behind?

It is worth it. Quick post-storm patches often fixed the missing shingles while broken seal strips and bent flashing elsewhere went unaddressed. A free roof inspection will show whether the rest of the roof came through intact.

What should I do about out-of-town roofers knocking on doors after a storm?

Take your time. Ohio law gives you until midnight of the third business day to cancel a home-solicitation contract. Don't sign over power of attorney, don't pay in full up front, and confirm the contractor will pull a permit from the right Delaware County office.

Does hail damage always mean a new roof?

No. Light hail may damage only gutters and vents, or a few shingles that can be repaired. Heavier hail that fractures shingles across whole slopes usually justifies replacement. Our documentation shows your adjuster exactly which it is.
