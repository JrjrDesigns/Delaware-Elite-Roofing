Housing stock

A fast-growing township with roofs entering their first replacement cycle

Liberty Township, which includes the City of Powell, has Sawmill Parkway, SR-315, US-23, Home Road, Liberty Road and Hyatts Road carrying most of its traffic. Counting Powell, the township had 33,252 residents in 2020; the unincorporated township alone grew from 9,182 in 2000 to 14,581 in 2010 and 18,271 in 2020, and Delaware County's planners call it the third-fastest-growing township in the county. The Columbus Zoo and Aquarium is here too, at Riverside Drive and Powell Road on O'Shaughnessy Reservoir.

That growth came in waves of subdivisions. Olentangy Falls, near Hyatts and Taggart roads with Delaware mailing addresses, was a 2011 Parade of Homes site with ten builders, including M/I, Fischer, Rockford and Dominion. Liberty Trace, Wedgewood Park, Clark Shaw Reserve and the Hyatts Crossing condominiums add to the mix, and building hasn't stopped — the township saw 301 single-family permits in 2021 alone.

For roofs, the key number is age. Homes from the Olentangy Falls era are now about fifteen years old, which is when production-builder roofs typically start showing their first real wear: pipe-boot collars cracking, ridge caps loosening, and the first patches of granule loss on the sunniest slopes. Those problems are usually fixable with targeted roof repair, and catching them early can add years before a full replacement. Our guide to builder-grade roof lifespan explains what separates a roof that needs attention from one that's done.

On the job

In our experience with Liberty Township's 2010s-era homes , the first leak on a production-built roof is very often a plumbing vent, not the shingles. The rubber collar around the pipe dries out and splits after enough Ohio summers and winters, and water runs down the pipe into a bathroom ceiling. Homeowners assume they need a new roof; most of the time they need a new boot and a careful look at the rest of the penetrations while we're up there.

Weather exposure

One-acre lots, open fields and central Ohio wind

About 71% of Liberty Township's acreage is zoned FR-1, the one-acre-minimum farm residence district, and much of it was farmland not long ago. Big lots are part of the appeal, but they also mean fewer neighboring houses and mature trees between your roof and the wind. Open exposure is hard on shingle seal strips, ridge caps and the edges of the roof where wind gets underneath.

Central Ohio's wind history backs that up. The June 29, 2012 derecho brought gusts of 70–80+ mph across the region, and the remnants of Hurricane Ike on September 14, 2008 produced a 75 mph gust at the Columbus airport, a record for that station at the time. In March 2024, an EF1 tornado with winds up to 110 mph crossed central Delaware County through Berlin Township, just a few miles away. When a big wind event rolls through, use our after-the-storm roof checklist, and if shingles are missing or water is coming in, call (740) 555-0177 for emergency roof tarping.

Winter is the other half of the story. Columbus-area January temperatures average 29.6°F, with about 28 inches of snow a year and long stretches hovering around freezing. The Residential Code of Ohio requires an ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line, and we install it on every replacement — it's one of the details that separates a code-compliant re-roof from a quick overlay.

On the job

When homeowners on Liberty Township's larger lots ask us why the shingles on one side of the house wear faster than the other, the answer is usually the field next door. On a lot with open ground to the west, that side of the roof takes most of the wind and afternoon sun with nothing to soften either. We'll often find lifted tabs and heavier granule loss on that one exposure while the opposite slope still looks nearly new — which is useful, because it can mean a repair now instead of a full roof.

Permits

Liberty Township, Powell or Delaware? Getting the permit right

Liberty Township is a patchwork of mailing addresses. A house can have a Powell or Delaware address and still sit in unincorporated Liberty Township, and a house that looks like it's in the township may actually be inside Powell's city limits — Golf Village, for instance, was annexed into Powell. The mailing address doesn't decide who permits your roof; the jurisdiction does.

For unincorporated Liberty Township, residential building permits come from Delaware County Building Safety. Inside Powell, the City of Powell requires a permit for all re-roofing, with photo documentation of the ice guard, underlayment, flashing and valleys, and requires the new roofing material to match what was previously installed unless structural changes are made. Statewide, the Ohio Board of Building Standards treats re-roofing as work that needs plan approval unless it's a minor repair. We check your parcel before we quote and pull the permit from the correct office — our Delaware County re-roof permit guide lays out who handles what.

Some Liberty Township subdivisions may also have HOAs that review exterior changes. If yours does, we'll prepare the color and product submittal as part of our HOA-approved roof replacement service.

Frequently asked — Liberty Township

My mailing address says Powell. Does the City of Powell permit my roof?

Only if your home is inside Powell city limits. Many Powell and Delaware mailing addresses are actually in unincorporated Liberty Township, where Delaware County Building Safety issues the permit. We check the parcel and pull the right permit.

My Olentangy Falls-area home is about 15 years old. Does it need a new roof?

Not necessarily. Around fifteen years is when production-built roofs often need their first repairs — vent boots, ridge caps, flashing. A free roof inspection tells you whether repairs will carry it for years or whether replacement makes more sense.

Why do large Liberty Township lots seem to get more wind damage?

About 71% of the township is zoned for one-acre-minimum lots, much of it former farmland with few trees to break the wind. More exposure means more stress on seal strips and roof edges. Call (740) 555-0177 after any big wind event.

Do you install an ice barrier on Liberty Township re-roofs?

Yes. The Residential Code of Ohio requires an ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line, and we install it on every roof replacement.

Can you help with an insurance claim after a storm in Liberty Township?

Yes. We document damage with photos and measurements for your adjuster. Our roof insurance claims guide explains Ohio's claim timelines.
