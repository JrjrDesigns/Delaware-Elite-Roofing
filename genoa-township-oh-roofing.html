Housing stock

Genoa Township's roofs went up all at once — and they're aging out together

Genoa Township sits in southeastern Delaware County, with Hoover Reservoir taking up a large share of the township and much of its southern end — from the Franklin County line north to Maxtown Road — annexed into Westerville over the years. The 2020 census counted 24,806 residents, and the township reports 8,923 housing units with a median home value of $437,800. That's a lot of roof area, and much of it was built in a remarkably short window.

The township's own history spells it out: between 1998 and 2003, 3,810 new residential dwelling units were permitted, adding more than 7,000 residents and nearly tripling the population. Permits peaked at 716 in 2002. Every one of those houses is now somewhere between 23 and 28 years old, and production homes of that era were typically finished with builder-grade asphalt shingles. We break down why that generation of roof is failing now in our guide to builder-grade roof lifespan in Delaware County subdivisions.

Highland Lakes, which straddles Genoa Township and Westerville near Polaris Parkway, is a good example of the township's housing. It's mostly 1990s and early-2000s homes around a 27-hole Jack Nicklaus course — brick colonials to ranches on half-acre to acre-plus lots — with active homeowners associations. Bigger homes on bigger lots often carry bigger, more cut-up roofs: more valleys, more step flashing where roof planes meet walls and dormers, and more places for a quarter-century-old shingle to give up first.

The practical result is that a roof in Genoa Township rarely fails all at once. It wears unevenly — south- and west-facing slopes first, valleys and penetrations next — and a good inspection tells you whether you're looking at a targeted roof repair or a full replacement. Our repair-or-replace walkthrough during a free roof inspection is built around exactly that question.

On the job

We notice a pattern on Genoa Township streets — because so many homes on a street were built within a year or two of each other, the roofs tend to fail in clusters. When one house on a cul-de-sac gets a new roof, the neighbors usually start noticing the same curled tabs and bare granule patches on their own houses within a season or two. When we're inspecting one of those homes, we often tell the owner the neighbor's tear-off is a useful preview of what's under their own shingles — same era, same type of builder-grade product, same exposure.

Weather exposure

Wind, open water and central Ohio winters

Genoa Township's biggest geographic feature is also its biggest roof factor. Hoover Reservoir covers 3,272 acres and stretches about eight miles, and homes that sit along open water or open fields don't get the wind break that mature trees and neighboring rooflines provide deeper in a subdivision. Wind can break a shingle's adhesive seal strip without tearing it off, leaving a tab that lifts in every gust afterward.

Central Ohio has seen its share of serious wind events. The June 29, 2012 derecho brought gusts of 70–80+ mph across the region, and the remnants of Hurricane Ike on September 14, 2008 produced a 75 mph gust at the Columbus airport, a record for that station at the time. Closer to home, the March 14, 2024 EF1 tornado tracked roughly 36 miles across Delaware County — through Berlin Township and near Sunbury and Galena — damaging at least 254 homes and businesses. Genoa homeowners who heard about damage a few miles north have good reason to get a post-storm roof inspection after the next big wind event rather than waiting for a ceiling stain.

Winter matters too. Columbus averages a January mean temperature of 29.6°F and about 28 inches of snow a year, with temperatures that hover around freezing for weeks. That's why the Residential Code of Ohio requires an ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line — and why we check for it on every Genoa re-roof. Permits & HOAs

Who permits your Genoa Township roof?

This is the question Genoa homeowners ask us most, because the township's history of annexation means two houses a mile apart can answer to different offices. Genoa Township itself says building permits are issued by the Delaware County Department of Building Safety, and the township's own Residential Improvement Permit covers things like additions, decks and fences — roofing isn't on that list. Homes with Westerville addresses that sit inside the annexed areas go through the City of Westerville instead.

Either way, a re-roof isn't something to do off the books. The Ohio Board of Building Standards has stated that plan approval is required for reroofing projects on one-, two- and three-family dwellings unless the work is a minor repair, and Delaware County's own building code requires a permit to alter, repair or replace. The Residential Code of Ohio also caps re-roofing at two layers — if your house already has two, everything comes off. We pull the permit from the correct office for your address; our Delaware County re-roof permit guide explains how the county, Delaware city, Powell and Westerville split it up.

If you live in Highland Lakes or another Genoa neighborhood with an active HOA, check your association's exterior-change rules before you sign anything. We can't speak to any one association's roof standards without seeing them, but we routinely put together color and material packets for review — see our HOA-approved roof replacement process.

On the job

When homeowners in Genoa Township ask us whether they can just replace the one slope that's failing, we usually find the answer depends on how old the rest of the roof is, not just how it looks. On a 25-year-old builder-grade roof, the 'good' slopes are often only a few years behind the bad one, and the new shingles won't match the faded originals. We'll show you photos of every slope so you can decide — sometimes a section repair buys real time, and sometimes it's money spent twice.

Frequently asked — Genoa Township

Why are so many Genoa Township roofs being replaced right now?

Genoa Township permitted 3,810 new homes between 1998 and 2003, peaking at 716 permits in 2002. Those original builder-grade roofs are now 23–28 years old, which is the end of the road for many of them. Our builder-grade roof lifespan guide explains the signs to look for.

My address says Westerville but I thought I was in Genoa Township. Which office permits my roof?

It depends on whether your lot is in unincorporated Genoa Township (Delaware County Building Safety) or in an area annexed into Westerville (City of Westerville). We confirm the jurisdiction before we schedule the job and pull the permit ourselves — call (740) 555-0177 with your address.

Does Highland Lakes require HOA approval for a new roof?

Highland Lakes has active homeowners associations, so check your association's exterior-change rules before a replacement. We haven't verified any one association's roof standards, but we prepare color and material submittals routinely — see our HOA roof replacement page.

Are homes near Hoover Reservoir harder on roofs?

Homes facing open water or open land generally take more direct wind than homes sheltered by trees and neighboring houses, which can break shingle seal strips over time. A roof inspection checks for lifted tabs that look fine from the ground.
