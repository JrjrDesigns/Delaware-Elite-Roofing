Housing stock

Newer homes on open ground in the middle of the county

Berlin Township sits in the center of Delaware County — 25.4 square miles, including 3.1 square miles of water, with part of the City of Delaware in its northwest corner. The 2020 census counted 9,344 residents. Much of what's being built here is new construction from national builders: new traditional homes with farmhouse and neoclassical influences. Berlin Manor, with Delaware mailing addresses and Olentangy schools, is one example, and part of Evans Farm — the Parkside section — falls inside the township as well.

New houses don't mean roof-proof houses. Production homes are typically finished with builder-grade architectural shingles, and many of these subdivisions sit on former farmland with few mature trees between the houses and the open fields. That combination — new shingles whose seal strips are still setting up, and nothing to break the wind — is exactly where we look hardest after a storm. If your home is only a few years old, a roof inspection now gives you a documented baseline while the builder's warranty may still apply.

The township isn't all subdivisions, either. National Weather Service surveyors recorded barns destroyed here in 2024, and we're happy to look at outbuilding roofs while we're on site, including metal roofing options for structures that need a durable, low-maintenance cover.

On the job

In our experience with Berlin Township's newer homes , the owner usually doesn't know what the roof looked like on day one — so after a storm, nobody can say for sure what's new damage and what was there at closing. We encourage owners of homes built in the last few years to let us photograph every slope, ridge and penetration while the roof is still young. It costs nothing, and if a storm or a warranty question comes up later, there's a dated record of what the roof looked like before.

Storm history

March 14, 2024: the EF1 tornado through Berlin Township

Berlin Township was in the direct path of the March 14, 2024 tornado. According to the National Weather Service in Wilmington, the EF1 tornado formed near US-23 south of Delaware shortly before 9 p.m. on March 14, 2024, strengthened after crossing US-23 and tracked through Berlin Township before crossing the Olentangy River and continuing near Sunbury and Galena. Its path ran roughly 36 miles with peak winds of 110 mph and a width of up to 600 yards.

Near Olentangy Berlin High School, transmission towers were knocked down. NWS surveyors documented homes with siding and roof damage and barns that were destroyed, and in some neighborhoods winds of 95–105 mph caused windows to blow out and partial roof removal. The Delaware Gazette reported at least 254 homes and businesses affected countywide, 25 with major damage, and a state emergency declaration. No one was injured.

Here's why that still matters in 2026: plenty of Berlin Township roofs were patched quickly that spring, and plenty more were never inspected because nothing was visibly missing. High wind can break shingle seal strips, crease tabs, loosen ridge caps and pull flashing without leaving anything in the yard. Those roofs leak months or years later, and by then it's harder to tie the damage to a specific storm. Our after-the-storm checklist covers what to look for, and our storm damage roof repair team handles the documentation adjusters ask for. If a future storm opens your roof, we provide 24/7 emergency roof tarping — call (740) 555-0177.

On the job

When Berlin Township homeowners show us a roof that was patched after the 2024 tornado , we look at the patch as closely as the damage. Repairs made right after a storm are often done fast, with whatever shingles are on hand. We regularly check whether replacement shingles were hand-sealed, whether nails landed in the right zone, and whether the repaired area matches the slope around it. A patch that was fine for getting through the spring isn't always one you want carrying the roof for the next decade.

Permits & HOAs

Permits, HOAs and insurance timelines in Berlin Township

Residential building permits in Berlin Township are issued by Delaware County Building Safety, which covers all of the county's townships. The Ohio Board of Building Standards has stated that plan approval is required for reroofing one-, two- and three-family dwellings unless the work is a minor repair, and the county's building code requires a permit to alter, repair or replace. If your home is in the part of the City of Delaware that lies within the township, the Delaware City Building Department handles it instead. Our Delaware County re-roof permit guide explains the split, and we pull the permit for you either way.

Newer subdivisions such as Berlin Manor have homeowners associations, and HOAs commonly want to approve shingle color and style before a replacement. We put together the submittal — product, color, sample photos — as part of our HOA-approved roof replacement service, so the association can sign off before tear-off day.

If you're filing a claim, Ohio's insurance rules (OAC 3901-1-54) require your insurer to acknowledge it within 15 days and accept or deny within 21 days of proof of loss, or explain why it needs more time. Many replacement-cost policies also set a deadline to complete repairs, so don't sit on the paperwork. Our Ohio roof insurance claims guide walks through the steps.

Frequently asked — Berlin Township

My Berlin Township house was near the March 2024 tornado path but nothing blew off. Should I still get it inspected?

Yes. The EF1 produced winds up to 110 mph through the township, and wind can break seal strips and crease shingles without removing them. A free inspection with photos tells you whether there's hidden damage — call (740) 555-0177.

My home is only a few years old. Isn't the roof still under the builder's warranty?

Builder and manufacturer warranties generally cover defects, not storm damage — storm damage is usually an insurance matter. Documenting your roof's condition now makes either conversation easier later. See our storm damage checklist.

Who issues roofing permits in Berlin Township?

Delaware County Building Safety issues residential permits for the township. Homes inside City of Delaware limits go through the Delaware City Building Department. We confirm the right office and pull the permit.

Can you repair barn and outbuilding roofs damaged by wind?

Yes. We inspect and repair outbuilding roofs and can quote metal roofing for barns and sheds that need a durable replacement.

Does my Berlin Township HOA need to approve a new roof?

Newer subdivisions such as Berlin Manor have HOAs, and associations commonly review exterior changes such as shingle color. Check your association's rules; we prepare the approval packet as part of every HOA roof replacement.
