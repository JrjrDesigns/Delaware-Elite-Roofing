Roofing for Delaware County Homeowners

Delaware County is not a suburb of anywhere — it is its own place, and its roofs have their own story. The county grew faster than any other in Ohio by percentage for three straight census decades, from about 67,000 residents in 1990 to more than 214,000 in 2020. That growth left a landscape of 1990s and 2000s subdivisions in the southern townships, historic homes and carriage houses in the City of Delaware, and small villages like Ashley and Ostrander that date to the mid-1800s.

Delaware Elite Roofing serves homeowners across that whole map. We handle full roof replacement, targeted roof repair and roof leak repair, storm damage documentation for insurance, gutters and free roof inspections. We are licensed and insured, we put every estimate in writing with photos, and we answer storm calls 24/7 at (740) 555-0177.

Every inspection ends the same way: you see photos of every slope, we explain what we found in plain language, and we tell you whether the roof needs a repair, a replacement or nothing at all. If a repair will buy you years, we say so. If an insurance claim is not justified, we say that too. Read more about how we work, or browse answers to common questions in our roofing FAQ.

Aging roofs

The Builder-Grade Roofs of the Boom Are Aging Out

The numbers explain why so many Delaware County roofs are due at once. Genoa Township permitted 3,810 new homes between 1998 and 2003. Orange Township's building permits peaked at 762 in 2004. Tartan Fields and Scioto Reserve in Concord Township broke ground in 1997 and 1998. Roofs installed in those years are now roughly 20 to 28 years old, and most were the builder's entry-level shingle package.

Those roofs did their job, but curling tabs, granules in the gutters and failed pipe boots are showing up across the county. Our guide to builder-grade roof lifespan explains the warning signs, and our roof replacement cost guide gives realistic 2026 price ranges so you can plan before a leak forces the decision.

On the job

When homeowners in Lewis Center ask us whether a few missing shingles mean a whole new roof, the honest answer often comes down to color. Many roofs from the 2004 building peak were installed in shingle lines and shades that have since been discontinued, so even a careful patch shows up as a different-colored square on the front slope. If the rest of the roof is healthy, we will repair it and tell you what to expect visually. If it is near the end anyway, we will show you the photos and let you decide whether the patch is worth it.

Storms

Storm Damage, Including the 2024 Tornado

On March 14, 2024, an EF1 tornado with peak winds of 110 mph formed near US-23 south of Delaware and tracked roughly 36 miles through Berlin Township and past Sunbury and Galena. At least 254 homes and businesses were affected, and the state declared an emergency. Smaller tornadoes touched down near Delaware Lake in 2016 and east of Delaware in 2020, and the whole region has weathered the 2008 Ike windstorm and the 2012 derecho.

After a storm, we inspect every slope, photograph damage for your insurer and tarp open roofs fast with emergency roof tarping. Our storm damage checklist and Ohio roof insurance claims guide walk you through the steps and the deadlines Ohio insurers have to meet. If water is coming in, do not wait for business hours — storm calls are answered around the clock.

On the job

In our experience with the open lots in Berlin and Liberty townships, wind damage starts at the edges. Subdivisions built on former farmland have few mature trees to slow a gust, so the rake edges and ridge caps on the windward side take the beating while the middle of the roof looks untouched. We check those edges by hand on every storm inspection, because that is where a small lifted shingle turns into a leak a season later.

Paperwork handled

HOA Approvals and Permits, Handled

A Delaware County re-roof can involve several different permit offices — Delaware County Building Safety for the townships and villages, the City of Delaware, Powell or Westerville — depending on where your parcel sits. Powell requires photo documentation of the ice guard, underlayment and flashing. Downtown Delaware's historic district requires a Certificate of Appropriateness before roof work starts. Our Delaware County roof permit guide shows which office covers your address.

Many neighborhoods also have design review. Scioto Reserve's HOA requires approval before any roof replacement, and its original community shingle color is Driftwood/Weathered Wood. Our HOA-approved roof replacement service prepares the color sheets and application so your approval is in hand before the dumpster arrives.

Frequently asked

Do you offer free roof inspections in Delaware County?

Yes. We inspect every slope, the flashing, vents, gutters and attic, and give you photos and a written assessment at no charge. Learn more about our roof inspections or call (740) 555-0177.

My house was built in the early 2000s. Is it time for a new roof?

Possibly. Roofs from the 1998–2008 building boom are now around 18 to 28 years old, and most started as builder-grade shingles. Age alone doesn't decide it — condition does. Our builder-grade roof lifespan guide lists the signs to look for.

Was my neighborhood affected by the March 2024 tornado?

The National Weather Service tracked the EF1 from south of Delaware through Berlin Township and near Sunbury and Galena. Homes near the path may have loosened shingles that aren't visible from the ground. See our storm damage checklist.

Will you get my HOA's approval before replacing the roof?

Yes. We prepare color samples, product specifications and the application for your HOA's design board, then schedule the work once approval is in hand.

Who pulls the roofing permit?

We do. We confirm whether your parcel falls under Delaware County Building Safety, the City of Delaware, Powell or Westerville and file with the right office.

How much does a new roof cost in Delaware County?

Most full asphalt replacements in central Ohio fall roughly between $8,000 and $18,000, depending on size, pitch and materials. Our roof replacement cost guide breaks it down, and a free inspection gives you the real number.
