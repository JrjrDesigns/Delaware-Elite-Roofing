Know before you spend

Why a roof inspection is the first step

Most roofs fail slowly and quietly. Shingles lose granules, seal strips let go, flashing loosens and a pipe boot cracks, and none of it shows from the driveway until water appears on a ceiling. In Delaware County, two things make an inspection especially worthwhile right now: the enormous number of roofs from the 1998–2008 subdivision build-out that are reaching the end of their service life, and the storm record – three tornadoes in or near the county since 2016, including the March 14, 2024 EF1 that crossed it from south of Delaware to beyond Galena.

Our inspections are free and come with photos of everything we find. You will get an honest answer to the question that actually matters: does this roof need nothing, a repair, or a replacement – and how soon? Call (740) 555-0177 to schedule.

Checklist

What we check on every inspection

We get on the roof whenever it is safe to walk, and into the attic whenever it is accessible. A ground-level look with binoculars cannot tell you whether seal strips are holding.

  • Shingle condition on every slope: granule loss, cracking, curling, brittleness
  • Seal strip adhesion, tested by gently lifting tabs along rakes, ridges and corners
  • Hail bruising and dents on vents, gutters and flashing
  • Step, counter, chimney and valley flashing; kick-out flashing at wall intersections
  • Pipe boots, box vents, ridge vents and exhaust caps
  • Ridge and hip caps
  • Soft spots in the decking
  • Attic: daylight, staining, wet insulation, ventilation and signs of excess heat or moisture
  • Gutters, fascia, soffits and downspout discharge
  • Number of existing layers, which matters because Ohio's code bars adding a covering over two or more
On the job

When we inspect roofs near the 2024 tornado track that look fine from the ground, the seal-strip test is where the story changes. A shingle that was lifted by the wind and dropped back down looks normal, but when we gently raise the tab it comes up with no resistance – the adhesive bond is gone. One loose shingle is a repair. When we find unsealed tabs running along an entire rake or the windward side of a slope, that is storm damage the homeowner had no way of seeing, and it gets photographed shingle by shingle.

Types

Inspections for every situation

After a storm. If wind or hail has come through, an inspection documents any damage with dated photos before you contact your insurer. Under OAC 3901-1-54, once you file, your insurer must acknowledge the claim within 15 days and accept or deny it within 21 days of proof of loss. Our storm damage roof repair service takes it from there, and the storm damage checklist covers what to do in the first 48 hours.

Aging subdivision roofs. If your home was built during the big growth years – Genoa Township permitted 3,810 homes from 1998 to 2003, and Orange Township's permits peaked at 762 in 2004 – your original roof is likely somewhere between 18 and 28 years old. An inspection tells you whether you can plan a replacement for next year or need to act this season. Our guide on why 1998–2008 subdivision roofs are failing now explains what we look for.

Buying or selling. A roof condition report with photos answers the questions a buyer's home inspector will raise and helps sellers decide whether to repair, replace or price accordingly.

New construction. Delaware County is still building, from new subdivisions in Berlin Township to the fast-growing village of Ostrander. A newer roof can still have installation defects or storm damage, and it pays to document its condition while any builder or workmanship coverage still applies.

Older and historic homes. The City of Delaware has more than 500 homes and carriage houses on the National Register in its Historic Northwest Neighborhood, and the village cores of Sunbury, Galena and Ashley date to the 1800s. Roofs on these houses have often been repaired and altered many times, so we look closely at chimneys, old flashing, additions tied into the original roof, and how many layers are already there. If the property is in Delaware's Downtown Historic District, any roof work needs a Certificate of Appropriateness before it starts, and we flag that in the report. Our default on older houses is repair-first, and the inspection is how we figure out what can be saved.

On the job

When homeowners in Highland Lakes and the rest of Genoa Township ask us for a pre-listing inspection, the conversation usually turns to timing. The 1990s and early-2000s roofs in that part of the county are old enough that a buyer's inspector will almost always flag them, and a seller who learns that during a negotiation has very little leverage. Getting the roof looked at before the house is listed gives the owner time to repair, replace or price it in – on their terms instead of the buyer's.

Local knowledge

Inspections that account for where you live

A roof inspection here should account for more than shingles. If a replacement is likely, we tell you which building department will permit it – Delaware County Building Safety for every township and for Sunbury, Galena, Ashley and Ostrander; the City of Delaware, Powell or Westerville for addresses inside those cities. In Powell, every re-roof needs a permit, and the city asks for photos of the underlayment, ice guard at the eaves, flashing and valleys. Our Delaware County roof permit guide has the details.

If you live in a community with a design review board – Scioto Reserve in Concord Township, for example, where roof replacements must be approved before installation – we note the approval step in our report so it does not catch you by surprise. Our HOA roof replacement service handles that submittal.

We also note exposure. A home backing onto open fields or a reservoir is going to take more wind than one on a sheltered street, and that shapes our recommendation on everything from shingle choice to nailing pattern.

Frequently asked

Is your roof inspection really free?

Yes. Residential roof inspections are free, with photos, and there is no obligation to hire us for anything we find. Call (740) 555-0177 to schedule.

How often should I have my roof inspected?

A good rule of thumb is every few years for a newer roof, every year once it passes about 15 years, and after any major wind or hail event. Many Delaware County subdivision roofs are now in that annual-check window.

Can you inspect a roof that's too steep to walk?

Yes. We use roof anchors and fall protection on steep pitches and supplement with close-up photography where walking is not safe. We will not cut corners on safety to save time.

Will an inspection tell me whether I have an insurance claim?

It tells you whether there is storm damage and documents it with photos. Whether it is covered depends on your policy and the adjuster. Our roof insurance claims guide explains the Ohio process.

I'm selling my house in Delaware County. Should I inspect the roof first?

If the roof is more than 15 years old, yes. A pre-listing inspection lets you address issues on your own schedule rather than during a buyer's negotiation.

Do you inspect the attic too?

Whenever there is safe access. The attic shows leaks, ventilation problems and decking damage that cannot be seen from outside, and it is often where we find the real story behind a ceiling stain.
