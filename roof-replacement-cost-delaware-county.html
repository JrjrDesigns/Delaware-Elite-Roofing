The short answer

What a Roof Replacement Typically Costs in Delaware County

No one publishes a Delaware County-only roofing price survey, so the honest starting point is central Ohio pricing as a whole. Published 2024–2026 surveys of Columbus-area jobs put a typical asphalt-shingle replacement at roughly $4 to $6 per square foot of roof surface installed — about $400 to $600 per "square" (100 square feet). On a 2,000-square-foot roof surface that works out to roughly $7,800 to $12,200. Most full replacements in the region land somewhere between $8,000 and $18,000, and a mid-size home commonly falls in the $11,000 to $15,000 band.

Where a Delaware County home lands within those ranges depends on factors we cover below: two-story height, steeper pitches, homeowner-association design rules and which of several permit offices covers the address. Remember that roof square footage is not the same as your home's floor area. A two-story colonial in Genoa Township with a 2,600-square-foot floor plan may have a smaller roof than a sprawling ranch in Liberty Township, while a home with several gables, a bonus room and a turret can carry far more roof than its floor plan suggests.

Treat every number on this page as a planning range. The only figure that matters is the one built from your roof's measurements, pitch and condition, which is exactly what our free roof inspection produces. Call (740) 555-0177 and we will measure, photograph and price it in writing.

Local cost drivers

Why Delaware County Quotes Run Higher or Lower

Two roofs with the same square footage can be thousands of dollars apart. These are the factors we see move Delaware County quotes the most:

  • Two-story height and pitch. On a two-story home, especially one with an 8/12 or steeper main roof, the crew needs roof jacks, harness anchors and slower, more careful work, and debris has to come down farther. Expect a higher per-square price than a walkable ranch.
  • Complexity. Every valley, hip, dormer, skylight and wall intersection adds flashing labor and waste. Stacked gables over a garage add up quickly.
  • Layers. The Residential Code of Ohio (R908.3.1.1) does not allow a new covering over a roof that already has two or more layers, so a second layer means a full tear-off. Tear-off typically adds $1,000 to $3,000.
  • Code items. Ohio requires an ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line. A quote that leaves it out is not a cheaper quote — it is an incomplete one.
  • Decking. Rotten or delaminated sheathing is only visible after tear-off. A good contract prices replacement sheets per piece up front.
  • HOA color and profile rules. If your association reviews shingle color or requires a dimensional profile, you may not be able to choose the lowest-cost product.
  • Permits. Every Delaware County re-roof needs building-department approval. Fees and paperwork vary by office, and we build them into the quote.

HOA neighborhoods

How HOA Design Review Affects the Price

A large share of Delaware County homes sit inside associations with design review. In Concord Township, the Scioto Reserve HOA states that all roof replacements must be applied for and approved before installation, and its rules note that the original community shingle color is Driftwood/Weathered Wood, with any color change needing Design Review Board approval. Evans Farm in Lewis Center requires its Design Review Committee to approve exterior changes, and Golf Village in Powell has its own Design Review Board.

Design review rarely adds a big line item, but it does narrow the product list and adds a step before work starts. Our HOA-approved roof replacement service includes the manufacturer color sheets, specification pages and application paperwork, so approval time is built into the schedule instead of becoming a surprise.

Permits

Permit Costs and Which Office Charges Them

The Ohio Board of Building Standards treats re-roofing a one-, two- or three-family dwelling as requiring approval unless it is a minor repair, and every part of Delaware County falls under a certified building department. Who issues your permit depends on your address: Delaware County Building Safety for the townships and most villages, the City of Delaware for city addresses, Powell for Powell addresses and Westerville for annexed Westerville addresses. Powell, for example, requires photos of the felt, ice guard and flashing before final approval.

Permit fees are set by each office and change over time, so we price the actual fee into your quote rather than guess. Our Delaware County roof permit guide lays out which office covers which community.

Insurance-paid roofs

When Insurance Pays for the Roof

If wind or hail damaged the roof, your out-of-pocket cost may be closer to your deductible than the full price. The Ohio Department of Insurance notes that wind and hail deductibles can be a flat amount or a percentage of the dwelling's insured value — 2% of a $300,000 dwelling limit is $6,000, which surprises many homeowners. Replacement-cost policies also commonly require the work to be finished within a set window, such as 180 days, to collect the full amount.

We document storm damage with dated photos and measurements, and our Ohio roof insurance claims guide explains the timelines insurers must meet. We bill the real price of the job and expect you to pay your deductible as your policy requires.

Comparing bids

How to Read a Delaware County Roofing Quote

A fair comparison needs the same scope on every bid. Before you sign, make sure each quote states the shingle line and color, how many layers come off, the ice barrier and underlayment, drip edge, ventilation plan (ridge vent or box vents), new pipe boots and step flashing, the decking price per sheet, the permit, cleanup and magnet sweep, and the warranty terms in writing. If a contractor will not put those items on paper, the low number is not really a number.

On the job

In our experience pricing two-story homes in Orange and Genoa townships, the biggest gap between competing quotes is almost never the shingle — it is what got left off the page. One bid assumes a single layer when there are two, another skips the ice barrier at the eaves, a third never mentions the attic vents. By the time the missing pieces are added back, the "cheap" bid usually lands right next to the others, so we encourage homeowners to line the scopes up side by side before they compare totals.

Frequently asked

What does a two-story Delaware County home typically pay for a new roof?

Most two-story homes we price fall within the regional $8,000–$18,000 range, often toward the middle or upper end because of pitch, height and complexity. Steep 8/12-plus roofs need more safety setup and labor than walkable ranch roofs. A free inspection measures your roof and gives you a written number — call (740) 555-0177.

Does my HOA's color requirement make the roof more expensive?

Usually only slightly. Approved colors like Scioto Reserve's Driftwood/Weathered Wood standard are common architectural-shingle colors. The bigger effect is that design review can rule out the cheapest products. Our HOA-approved roof replacement service handles the paperwork.

Is the permit included in a Delaware County roofing quote?

It should be. We pull the permit from the correct office — County Building Safety, the City of Delaware, Powell or Westerville — and include the fee in your price. See our roof permit guide for which office covers your address.

Can I save money by installing new shingles over the old ones?

Only if the roof has a single existing layer, and even then we rarely recommend it, because the decking can't be checked and the new shingles telegraph every bump underneath. With two or more layers, Ohio's residential code requires a full tear-off.

How long is a roof quote good for?

Material prices move, so most written quotes are honored for a limited period stated on the quote. If your project depends on an insurance claim or HOA approval, tell us up front and we'll schedule around those steps.
